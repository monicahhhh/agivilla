Personal Transformation: From Algorithm "Black Box" to Operations' Fast Feedback

Monica

Bella is the co-founder of AskSia. Previously, she was responsible for user growth from 0 to 1, reaching 200,000 users and millions in ARR, most importantly with almost no advertising costs.

Bella was an algorithm engineer before, transitioning from technology to operations. Today, we'll talk about Bella's story, starting with why she moved from tech to operations, then discussing her growth experiences.

Bella

Hello everyone, I'm Bella. I started studying CS in university, then worked on AI-related topics, and my master's research was also in AI algorithms. Later, I joined a large company, working at Huawei on algorithms for hardware-related applications. After two years, I started the AskSia project. I feel that transitioning to operations was a natural progression. Developing AI applications is very dependent on operations. A product must first be good, but if no one sees it, it's difficult to commercialize.

I personally had to build this product from 0 to 1, acquire more paying users, and drive growth. So I focused more on operations, including early-stage channel operations, product growth, and adjusting pricing strategies. These are the areas where I invested more effort.

Monica

What do you think is different between working in operations and being an algorithm engineer?

Bella

Transitioning to operations has been quite interesting; it provides much faster feedback than working with algorithms. With algorithms, you often have to do a lot of research, see which algorithms can be used, and then test them. Sometimes you spend two or three days without any results, or debugging leads to inexplicable non-optimization, or inexplicably it just optimizes. It feels very much like a "black box."

Unlike operations, where I can get results quickly. I post a video or a tweet, and I can get thousands of registered users or thousands of likes. The feedback is much faster than with algorithms, and I really enjoy this kind of work state.

Product Positioning: Solving Three Major Learning Pain Points for Global University Students

Monica

Bella, could you please introduce AskSia and tell us what kind of product it is and who its target audience is?

Bella

AskSia is an AI education tool product, essentially targeting university students worldwide. From the beginning, our positioning was to serve the global market, addressing some of the most common learning pain points students encounter during their studies. In a nutshell, it helps university students excel in their courses. This is the biggest problem we aim to solve.

We've been operating for over a year, and we have paying users in North America, the US, Canada, the UK, all of Europe, Australia, Japan, Korea, and Southeast Asia. Globally, in about a year, we've accumulated nearly 200,000 registered users.

We achieved millions in ARR in just over a year.

Monica

What's the approximate conversion rate to paid users?

Bella

That's quite interesting. Payment conversion is related to product pain points, and also very much to the channels you choose. Because we do a lot of content marketing.

For example, when a user sees the product, they might be scrolling on their phone and discover a product that can help them write assignments better. When they're rushing an assignment, they save this tool and can immediately go to the website to use it to help them with their homework.

First, they're "planted" with the idea, then they pay for the product. This path is very smooth because the demand frequency is very high. So, our overall paid conversion rate, from UV to paid, is roughly 5-10%. Some individual regions even exceed 10%.

Monica

For a B2C product, a 10% paid conversion rate is excellent.

What core problem does the product solve for users?

Bella

There are three main types of pain points.

The first type, for example, science students studying math or physics, have many assignments that require explaining the logical thinking behind problems. If they encounter a problem they can't solve at all and have no clue, they're stuck. So, we introduced a feature that provides detailed explanations. Through these explanations, students can gain insights and better understand the problems. By solving this issue, we gained a significant number of paying users from science fields.

The second pain point, besides science students, is for humanities students, such as those studying business or marketing. They need to read articles more. We had an intern studying marketing whose professor often assigned hundreds of pages of documents, which was very painful for him. For these students, we created a feature that allows them to quickly see a summary of the article, and also provides references and even image summaries, helping them better grasp what the article is about. This solves a pain point for humanities students.

The third function, beyond science and humanities students, also addresses their pain point of not being able to keep up in class.

A teacher's class is one hour long, and some students can't keep up. For cross-language learners, once they fall behind, it becomes even harder to understand.

So we launched a feature that provides real-time transcription and translation during class. Many students take notes on it, and by combining uploaded class materials with the transcribed content, it can summarize them into a high-quality AI note. After class, they can review using these notes.

This scenario, launched in February and March of this year, helped us achieve a significant third wave of growth. We reached millions in ARR by March.

Monica

What's your pricing model?

Bella

Subscription is quite relevant to the demographic. Since we target university students, the pricing isn't very high, about ten dollars a month. For university students, it's really not that expensive. Think about it, a cup of coffee costs five dollars.

Monica

Did you have tiered pricing like monthly, quarterly, and annual subscriptions back then?

Bella

We didn't make it that complicated, mainly a monthly and an annual fee.

But we are also adjusting. Sometimes we might turn the annual fee into quarterly payments, or add a 1-day trial, 7-day trial, wanting to use payment conversion rate as a lever to see if we can improve it.

Growth Path: Zero-Cost Content Marketing and Scenario-Based User Acquisition

Monica

How did you achieve 0-1 PMF?

Bella

The first six months were spent finding direction. Initially, we targeted students taking AP exams, like AP US history, but we didn't find the right channels for this group. PMF itself means a product's pain point and features must serve the target users well, and you need to find the right channels where many paying users exist.

Within six months, we hadn't found the right channels. We tried many, even spending a lot of KOL budget on TikTok for advertising.

On TikTok, some students take AP A-levels, but essentially, Gen Z on TikTok is more numerous, yet still relatively small compared to university students. So, the overall ROI (Return on Investment) from those ads was completely wasted.

Later, we started thinking, are there larger scenarios that solve similar problems? Many subjects are in AP, and university students in their first or second year also take APs. AP also includes some science subjects, like math, which university students also do. So, we directly streamlined these into a tool product for university students, focusing on science majors.

At that time, we found that many science students really wanted such a tool. Including ourselves when we were students—I was a science student, studying CS required a lot of math. Sometimes I couldn't do homework, or before exams, I wanted to look at past exam questions but couldn't solve them and had to search for answers myself. This pain point was really significant.

So, we decided to focus on the science scenario, package this scenario into video content, and then tried a channel: Xiaohongshu. Our title was "Overseas Students' Homework Helper." When you saw this, you knew what it did. We used very direct and clear content to attract users, letting them know exactly what problems you could solve. Later, we also tested some scenario-based content. For example, we found that many people purchased our product at night.

Why at night? Because they were rushing deadlines and needed to submit assignments quickly. At this time, you'd find many more paying users than in the morning or daytime. We thought this might be a good scenario.

So we created many scenarios like this, packaged them into content, and attracted people who felt they had similar pain points.

Because people would think, "This content is my life, isn't this me?" So many people would save such content, feeling that it genuinely solved problems in their lives. That's one aspect.

Another aspect is, for example, during reading week, we found many people purchasing the product. A big reason is that during reading week, they have a lot of assignments to write but no one to ask. At this time, an AI tool for 24-hour real-time problem-solving becomes exceptionally important. This is a great scenario. We packaged important scenarios into content and put them on social media, and found that it really worked. In fact, there was no cost involved in the process because all the content was created by ourselves.

Operational Insights: Extracting Content Inspiration from User Behavior Data

Monica

How do you discover such precise demand points? Do you talk to a lot of people, or do you look at their communities?

Bella

There's a saying I think is very true: you should find the people who have already paid you, and ask why they paid you. Then amplify the scenarios in which they made a purchase.

For example, as I just mentioned, the time people buy our product is at night. If you delve deeper into users' pain points, you'll find they are the ones rushing assignments, and they've already paid you. That means many people are exactly like them, so you need to amplify the commonalities among these people and turn them into content.

Essentially, you need to look at your product's own data.

Monica

Do you do a lot of user interviews and follow-ups?

Bella

We look at user usage data and purchase data from a data perspective.

Monica

Because this type of product allows you to clearly see the user's onboarding process, what users are doing, and what problems they are solving, it's very clear. Generating content from user behavior is a particularly clever point.

What was your team configuration like back then?

Bella

Initially, the entire product and operations team was very young, mostly post-2000s. I think their biggest advantage is that they are very close to the users.

Many of our operations colleagues were students themselves. The content they created was essentially solving their own problems.

For example, I had a marketing intern who was an international student studying marketing. He did his undergraduate studies in China, and when he went abroad, he couldn't understand the classes at all. He really needed a product like this to help him with that problem. So, he was very close to the users, and I think this is a huge advantage for making this type of product.

Bella

Basically, for this type of product, you definitely need to add embedded tracking points.

Mainly, you look at the funnel: from UV to registration, you need to embed tracking points on the registration page; to payment, you also need to embed tracking points.

You also need to understand what questions users are asking.

This might help you better understand the proportion of your paying users or product users. Then, based on these proportions, I think it can better help you make decisions for your marketing.

Audience

How many people are on the team? How long did it take to develop?

Bella

The initial founding team was about five people. We had one full-time full-stack developer, one UI/UX designer, one product manager, and the rest were operations staff. We first built the product and then started promoting it.

Before reaching millions in ARR, we added many operations interns.

Monica

What's the distribution of your traffic sources? Organic traffic? Community? Paid acquisition?

Bella

Most of it is organic traffic. Because we've already figured out some content. For example, "Read your 12 weeks of PPTs in 2 hours," content like that, or hooks combined with scenarios, which can clearly help us gain traffic on social media. This applies to TikTok, Instagram, Xiaohongshu, and Douyin; we use the same hooks and scenarios, translated into different languages, and can get organic traffic.

Our main focus is on organic traffic. We also post some UGC, launched some accounts, and managed to get 600,000 organic traffic the next day, bringing in about two to three thousand registered users on that day.

Viral Content Dissection: How to Create Cross-Platform Viral Content

Monica

What kind of content can achieve 600,000 organic traffic the next day?

Bella

For this content, we used some KOC (Key Opinion Consumer) influencers to help us build matrix accounts. Because later, building matrix accounts heavily relies on content marketing. Besides creating content ourselves, we also collaborate with some excellent content creators.

First, for this product, since it targets university students, the influencer you choose should ideally be a university student themselves, which makes it more authentic. We have such KOCs who are university students, have tens of thousands of followers, and strong expressive abilities. With the effective hook scripts and creative directions we provide, they can quickly boost accounts, achieving 600,000 organic traffic exposure the next day, essentially without spending a penny.

Monica

Did this 600,000 exposure already exceed that KOC's daily average, with your creative empowerment?

Bella

Her account already had a lot of traffic and strong content creation ability.

The 600,000 exposure was partly because of her overall performance; she has a "Korean girl" style, very natural and lifelike. Plus, it might have been around final exam season, so people were more likely to watch such content and encounter it. If it were in July or August, this logic might not work because everyone is on holiday, and no one wants to watch this kind of stuff.

Audience

Is your income very cyclical?

Bella

I think for AI education products, they all face the same issue, including question banks.

During holidays, people aren't willing to study, so they won't use it. But during the back-to-school season, it's a huge surge again. All education products have this cycle; it's not a problem, it's a "situation."

Monica

What's your collaboration mechanism with KOC influencers?

Bella

We ask KOCs to help us build a matrix account and co-create content, working together on about 20-30 pieces of content per month.

Monica

Do you pay based on performance, or what kind of model is it?

Bella

Yes, but essentially there's always a base fee plus performance-based payment. These influencers are very willing to do it. Because KOCs like these usually take jobs one by one. If there's a product willing to maintain a long-term partnership, KOCs are very open to this collaboration model.

Do you study the recommendation algorithms of different platforms?

Bella

Now, all channels are starting to do short videos, including YouTube Shorts, which are transitioning from long-form videos to short-form. I think the content for short videos is actually quite similar. Because I found that if we put the same hooks and scenario content on different platforms, and even translate it into different languages, it can still work. We've also created many learning accounts in different languages.

Monica

In your PPT, you wrote that the platform for the Korean-speaking region is TikTok? I see all Western faces.

Bella

Why it became popular in Korea, I think it's because Koreans mostly see other Koreans. If Western faces appear in regions where Koreans usually scroll, it would be quite unique.

Monica

Did you directly use videos from European and American KOCs and AI translation?

One set of materials used across different language regions.

Bella

Yes, and we have an even more powerful hook: a very abstract side profile, combined with scary music, giving the feeling that finals are imminent.

Such videos, when posted on TikTok, Instagram, even different language matrix accounts—Korean, Taiwanese matrix accounts, as well as Xiaohongshu and Douyin—they all went viral.

Monica

One set of materials for multiple platforms, that's more efficient.

Did you determine this direction from the beginning, or did you transition to it later based on user behavior?

Initially, we planned to do AI education. It's just that at first, we didn't target university students, but rather high school students, like those taking AP exams. But the AP demographic is definitely much smaller than global university students; we actually targeted a smaller market.

Regarding channel selection, we also tried Reddit before, but it didn't work out. Ultimately, we chose a channel where I am very skilled in social media content marketing. But I believe Reddit is worth doing because it has a diverse range of people. However, Reddit also has high barriers; at the time, we couldn't make this channel work for AskSia.

Reddit is quite anti-marketing.

Bella

On Reddit, you have to write very softly. But if you write too softly, people won't know you're promoting a product, and you'll feel that the traffic has no value to you. But if you're too aggressive, you can easily get banned, so it's very difficult.

Scaling Challenges and Team Management

Audience

How do you transform content inspiration into a structured growth system?

Bella

Initially, when you create content and get these viral hits, it's essentially about testing.

See what popular memes or hooks are trending recently.

This requires your personal judgment to decide if such a hook can be combined with your product scenario. I think this needs a very intuitive logic; you need to judge with your "internet sense" whether this short video can go viral. But to scale it up, you can't rely purely on intuitive thinking.

You can dissect these viral hits. They consist of two parts: a hook and your product scenario. You can replicate this for different content creators to film.

Either go with UGC, have your KOCs film it; or your internal operations team continuously replicates such content. For example, our "2 hours to review 12 weeks of PPTs."

Monica

Is this hook still being used?

Bella

This pain point is very clear. "2 hours to read 12 weeks of PPTs" refers to our reading function, which is more for humanities-related content, promoting the scenario.

Through such a hook, we kept replicating this type of content. At that time, we replicated many pieces, all achieving similar results.

Moreover, such content very directly and simply expresses what the product can do and what value it can bring to our target users. On one hand, it might easily go viral, and on the other hand, its conversion rate will be very high. For example, a post with about a thousand likes can bring in nearly 100 paid conversions.

Monica

What is the user acquisition cost?

Bella

The operational acquisition cost we can achieve is around four dollars, which is a very low figure.

Mainly because our traffic is very precise, and the pain points we address are very clear, and the scenarios are very high-frequency. So, the overall paid conversion rate is high, and the effect of organic traffic is significant.

Monica

A four-dollar acquisition cost with a minimum first payment of ten dollars, breaking even on the first payment, that's quite impressive.

Audience

Is there a free version and an upgraded version?

Bella

I've seen many products that immediately ask you to pay, or bind your card right after registration, which is very commercial. Some of my friends do this, and their overall payment rate increased by 1 percentage point, which I think is very strong and definitely worth trying.

We tried it before too. But later we felt it was too aggressive; we still wanted to be friendly to students, so we provide a certain free quota.

I think the core is that product power can bring more conversions. If you believe the product is good enough and can truly help users solve problems, then you can let them experience the product first, so they genuinely feel it brings value. So, we also give some free access.

Monica

What is the extent of the free allowance? By time or by token?

Bella

Different scenarios might vary.

For example, for classes, it might revolve around a 1-hour class, allowing you to completely listen to a full class without it cutting off after 40 minutes, which would be very unfriendly.

So, we also consider user-friendliness when designing the free quota.

Audience

What limits the scale of zero-cost acquisition? Is there a ceiling to this? For example, at a very low acquisition cost, how much do you think you can achieve?

Bella

I think "zero cost" depends on platform trends, and it constantly changes.

The biggest bottleneck is that you have to constantly track what content is trending on platforms and follow trends very quickly. The challenge lies in quickly tracking trends and testing them.

There are also many automated parts here. For example, using some tools can help you better track what content is trending. The internal team has limited manpower for content creation, so later on, you still need to spend some advertising fees, for example, to find KOC influencers to become your content co-creators and help you build matrix accounts. Otherwise, it relies too heavily on the internal team's operational capabilities, and human resources are definitely limited.

Monica

Taking Xiaohongshu or Instagram as examples, what's the overall workflow?

Bella

First, you need to find corresponding personas based on the product itself. For example, if they are medical students, or CS students, different personas will use the product, and the scenarios will also be different. So, we choose different KOCs based on these personas. We also use tools to help us screen for influencers that better match our needs.

Normally, we send content in batches to influencers, sending emails to establish contact. The whole process is somewhat like finding KOLs. But because KOCs have lower unit costs, we choose KOCs. There are far more KOCs than KOLs, so the pool for selection is larger, allowing us to find more content creators. Later, we establish collaborations, manage these KOCs, have them post and create content, and co-create with them, providing some popular hooks and creative references.

Monica

Any tips on managing KOCs?

Bella

It still relies heavily on overall operations. Basically, if the KOCs are large in volume, like many teams in the AI education sector that have done very well, they use an operational management system. For example, managing these KOCs within a Discord community.

Monica

Do you also use Discord?

Bella

It depends on how large the KOC volume is. If it's less than 5, I don't think there's a need to use Discord.

Monica

How do you usually find KOCs?

Bella

For example, EasyKOL is a good tool for finding KOLs.

Essentially, the logic for finding KOCs and KOLs is the same. It's just that when screening, for KOLs, you look for over 100,000 followers, while for KOCs, you look for thousands or tens of thousands of followers, under 100,000.

On one hand, we choose influencers with potential, and on the other hand, if the budget is not high, we can pay based on performance. At that time, we also tried another method: finding many campus ambassadors, who also served as our content creators.

Regarding the settlement method for campus ambassadors, we give each person their own tracking link. One campus ambassador achieved over a hundred paid conversions in a month. We have a tracking link to monitor their revenue and then share a percentage with them.

Monica

How many such KOCs are needed to sustain continuous growth for a platform?

Bella

I personally think that only a few can truly bring in large traffic.

For example, we previously found over a dozen campus ambassadors, but only one or two truly brought in significant traffic and conversions, with excellent results. So, initially, you need to try many, keep the best ones, and turn them into more core members, making them feel closer to the product, collaborating more closely, and joining you. For instance, we give those core campus ambassadors labels like "Honorary Ambassador." They feel quite proud and honored to do such work.

Globalization and Cultural Adaptation Strategies

Monica

Speaking of cross-language region operations, including Europe, America, Japan, and Korea. What tips do you have for cultural adaptation?

Bella

Cross-language learners have a more urgent need for translation compared to local university students. However, their pain points are actually similar.

Both local students and cross-language learners watch YouTube. We also have a YouTube learning feature where you upload a YouTube link, and it summarizes it for you.

I even use this feature myself because I often watch YouTube to learn. With this feature, I can quickly understand what a 2-hour video is about, get a summary, and ask questions, which helps me understand the content better.

It's just that cross-language learners have more language barrier issues. But everyone has exams, assignments, and deadlines, so the scenarios are quite similar. There are just some things that might be a little different.

Monica

So your user base initially targeted Chinese international students, and then expanded to university students in other regions.

Bella

Yes. We initially focused on Xiaohongshu and Douyin channels, which had more international students. Later, we expanded to TikTok and Instagram, where we created matrix accounts for many countries, including North America, Korean, Japanese, and traditional Chinese accounts for Taiwan, reaching non-international student populations.

Because TikTok and Instagram have a huge user base, people all over the world use them. Koreans love to scroll, so if you create content there, you can reach these Koreans. So, it's channel-driven.

Monica

So, growth channels drove you to different target groups, rather than defining target groups first and then finding channels? Is that right?

Bella

We first created matrix accounts for some countries and found that it really worked.

Monica

The strategy is also the same, still one content system.

Throughout this process, has your product evolved in any way, along with your operational methods and growth?

Bella

The product can help find more leverage points.

If we only focused on problem-solving, we would quickly run out of steam because more and more products like that would emerge. So, we had to find new breakthroughs, which is why we looked for new scenario features.

Typically, we release a major version iteration every three months, and some minor feature iterations every month. We do this to find more breakthroughs that help us acquire paying users.

Industry Insights and Future Outlook

Monica

For this type of education and learning tool, these features don't sound very unique or special. For example, analyzing documents or solving problems could be replaced by GPT or other tools.

How do you view the development and ceiling of this product?

Bella

I think AskSia doing the all-in-one solution makes sense. For example, today in class, I have to use GPT, plus a meeting minutes tool, using two tools simultaneously, and they can't be connected. After transcription, I still have to import it into GPT, which is very troublesome.

The logic of doing all-in-one is to reduce this friction. For ten dollars, you can fully experience the three biggest pain points in learning. Otherwise, the user migration cost is too high. Using one product for a while, then another, and two products combined would cost over 50 dollars.

It's better to just spend ten dollars to do these three things.

I think the user migration cost is relatively high. Because on the product, users have a lot of historical usage data and class recording records. When they need to review, or during the final exam, they need the previous recordings to help them review.

So, the stronger the traces they leave on the product, the higher the migration cost. Many of our users did cancel their subscriptions during holidays, but when school started, they remembered and had to use AskSia again, and came back to renew. Perhaps the product itself has good retention, and users' own migration costs are relatively high, so overall user retention is also relatively high.

Audience

Is there a situation where a core feature is used frequently, while other features are not used much?

Bella

If users consistently use a particular feature, for example, if they came for the translation function, they will continue to focus on that. This was one of our questions at the time: how do we let users know about other features available on the product? But this can be optimized. For instance, upon entering the main page, users can quickly grasp what else the product can do.

The product needs to be simple enough so that users immediately know what they can do on the page; it shouldn't be too complex. If they can't find the entry points and it's too complicated, users won't know how to use other features.

Monica

Do your product and operations teams ever clash?

Bella

I think often. For example, the product team might think their product is excellent.

Previously, we developed a feature for online class attendees to input Zoom links, helping them manage their classes. However, this feature didn't meet expectations at the time, leading to some battles between product and operations. But we quickly launched a real-time in-class transcription and translation feature, which indeed helped us capture more users.

I don't think it's that there aren't enough people taking online classes, but rather that those attending live classes have a greater need for translation. Because users in that specific class scenario truly need a tool to help them translate what the teacher is saying in English or other languages, the paid conversion rate for this feature went up.

Monica

How do you effectively "build in public" for such a B2C product targeting specific demographics?

Bella

I think for B2C products, it's more about user mindset. For example, what we wanted to build at the time was: when you think of studying, you should think of AskSia. You need to directly and clearly implant this user mindset into potential users. Like those old, catchy advertising slogans, such as "Give gifts, give Brain Platinum."

I think this concept of user mindset itself requires constant, almost brainwashing, methods to make people realize: for studying, you should use your product, combining your scenarios with the product. This creates very deep user stickiness.

Monica

Finally, please give everyone a piece of advice, Bella.

Bella

Get users to pay as early as possible. This also allows you to test your product's PMF faster.

个人转型: 从算法“黑盒”到运营的快反馈

Monica

Bella之前是做算法工程师的,从技术转运营。今天我们一起来聊聊Bella的故事，先从她为什么会从技术转运营聊起，再讲讲她的增长经历。

Bella

大家好，我是Bella。我大学开始学CS，之后做跟AI相关的，硕士也是做AI算法的research。后来去了大厂，在华为做跟硬件相关的落地应用算法。做了两年之后，我就去做AskSia这个项目。我觉得转做运营这件事，是顺理成章的。做AI应用类产品，其实非常吃运营。一个产品首先得好用，但如果没人能看见，就很难商业化。

产品定位：解决全球大学生的三大学习痛点

Monica

Bella先帮我们介绍一下AskSia大概是个怎样的产品，面向什么样的群体。

Bella

AskSia是一个做AI教育类型的工具型产品，本质上面向全球大学生。一开始定位就是要做全球市场，解决学生在上学过程中最常见的一些学习痛点。用一句话说，就是帮大学生更好地上好一门课。这是我们想解决的最大问题。

我们做了一年多，北美、美国、加拿大、英国、整个欧洲、澳洲、日韩以及东南亚，我们都有付费用户。全球差不多一年多时间有将近二十多万注册用户，

花了一年多做到了一年百万美金的ARR。

Monica

付费率大概有多少？

Bella

这个挺有意思。付费一方面跟产品痛点有关，和你选择的渠道也很有关系。因为我们做内容营销比较多。

比方说，用户看到产品时，正好在刷手机，发现有个产品能帮他更好地写作业。赶作业的时候，他收藏这个工具，就能立刻到网站上来使用，帮他写作业。

先种草，再到产品付费。这条链路非常顺，因为需求频次非常高。所以我们整体的付费转化率，从UV到付费差不多有5%-10%。有些单地区甚至会超过10%。

Monica

对于 to C 的产品，付费转化率10%是非常好的。

产品核心解决的是用户哪一块的问题？

Bella

有三大类型的痛点。

第一大类型，比如理科学生，学数学、物理的，他们有很多作业，需要解释题目背后的逻辑思维。如果碰到完全不会解的题目，没有任何头绪，就没办法解决。所以我们当时出了一个功能，可以给出详细解释。通过详细解释，学生能得到一些思路，更好理解题目。通过解决这个问题，我们获得了非常多理科的付费用户。

第二方面的痛点，除了理科学生，还有文科学生，比如学business或marketing的。他们更需要读文章。我们有个实习生学marketing的，老师经常布置几百页的文档，对他来说非常痛苦。针对这类学生，我们做了一个功能，让他们能很快看到文章的summary，还会提供一些reference甚至图片summary，让他们更好地get到文章在讲什么。这是解决文科学生的痛点。

第三种功能，除了理科和文科学生，还能解决他们上课跟不上的痛点。

老师一节课1个小时，有些学生跟不上。对于跨语言学习者，一旦落下就更难听懂。

所以我们出了一个功能，上课时进行实时转录和翻译。很多学生在上面记笔记后，结合上课上传的材料和转录内容，能帮你总结成一份高质量的AI笔记。下课后可以根据笔记复习。

这个场景在今年2、3月份上线后，帮助我们获得了非常大的第三波增长。我们在3月份就已经做到了百万美金。

Monica

你们的收费模式是怎么样的？

Bella

订阅跟人群比较相关。因为我们定位的是大学生，定价没有很高，差不多十美金一个月。对大学生来说，真的没有那么贵。想想看，喝一杯咖啡都要五美金。

Monica

当时出了月度付费、季度付费和年费这种阶梯吗？

Bella

我们没有搞那么复杂，主要一个月度还有一个年费。

不过也在调整。有时候可能把年费变成季付，或者加上一些 1 day try、7 day try，想把付费转化率作为一个杠杆，看看能不能提高一下付费转化率之类的。

增长路径：零成本内容营销与场景化获客

Monica

你们当时0-1 PMF 是怎么跑通的？

Bella

前半年一直在找方向，一开始定位的是考AP的这群人，比如AP US history，因为这群人的渠道我们并没有找准。PMF本身，一个产品的痛点、功能，要服务好目标用户，还有你找好渠道，上面就有很多你的付费用户。

在半年内，我们并没有找准渠道。我们试了很多渠道，还花了很多KOL的预算在TikTok上面做投放。

TikTok上面有一些是选AP A-level的，本质上TikTok上面 Z世代的人比较多，但相比于大学生人数还是相对较小。所以当时整体投放下来的ROI（Return on Investment，投资回报率） 完全打水漂。

后来我们慢慢在想，有没有一些场景更大也是在解决类似的事情。考AP里面有很多学科，大学生在 大一大二也要去考AP，包括AP里面还有一些理科，比如数学，大学生也是要做的。后来我们把这些东西直接切成大学生的工具型产品，变成聚焦主打理科。

当时我们发现身边真的有很多学理科的学生很希望有这样的工具。包括我们自己以前上学的时候，我是理科生，学CS要学很多数学。当时有些作业不会做，或者在考试前想去看原来学生的一些past exam题目，没办法解决就得自己去搜， 这方面的痛点其实非常痛。

所以我们当时就想，要主打理科这个场景，把这个场景包装成视频内容，后来尝试了一个渠道，去尝试小红书。我们的标题就写“留学生的作业帮”，你看到这个东西就知道它在干什么。用很直接明了的内容去吸引用户，让他们知道你究竟能解决什么问题。后面我们也通过了一些场景内容的测试。比如我们发现，夜里有非常多的人购买我们的产品。

为什么在夜里？因为他们开始赶deadline，要赶紧交作业。这时候你会发现付费人数远比早上白天要多。我们觉得这可能是一个很好的场景。

我们就做了很多这方面的场景，把它包装成内容，吸引大家觉得有同样的痛点。

因为大家会觉得，这内容就是我的生活，这不就是我吗？所以就会有很多人收藏这样的内容，觉得它确确实实能在生活中解决问题。这是一方面。

还有一方面，比如在reading week的时候，我们发现有很多人在这期间购买产品。很大原因是在reading week有大量的作业需要写，但是无人可问，这时AI工具24小时实时解题工具就显得格外的重要，这是一个很好的场景。我们把重要的场景包装成内容，放到社交媒体上，发现它确实能跑通。实际上，中间的过程没有任何成本，因为所有的内容都是我们自己做的。

运营洞察：从用户行为数据中挖掘内容灵感

你怎么样去发现这种非常精准的需求点？是跟大量的人聊，还是说会去看他们的一些社区？

比方刚才讲的，购买我们产品的时间点就是在夜里。你深挖用户的痛点，会发现他是赶作业的这群人，他已经给你付费了。那说明有很多人跟他一模一样，你就要去放大这些人身上的共性，把它做成内容。

本质上要去看产品自己的一些数据。

会去做很多的用户访谈跟踪？

会从数据的角度去看用户的使用数据、购买的数据。

Monica

因为这一类的产品能够很明确地看到用户的Onboarding process，用户大概在做一些什么事情，解决什么问题，非常清晰。从用户行为上去生产内容，这个点特别巧妙。

60万的曝光，一方面是因为她整体的表现，她自己本身是“韩女”风格，表现非常自然，有生活气。再加上一点，当时正好可能是final季，大家会看这样的内容更多，更容易刷到。如果放到7、8月份，这套逻辑可能就不work了，因为大家都放假，没人愿意刷这种东西。

规模化挑战与团队管理

观众

如何把内容灵感转化成结构化的增长体系？

Bella

一开始去做内容，拿到这些爆款，本质上是要去测试的。

看最近有哪些很火的一些梗，有哪些很火的钩子。

这个东西通过你自己的个人判断觉得这样的钩子是否能够跟你的产品场景结合。我觉得这里需要一个很感性的逻辑，需要通过你自己的网感判断，看这个短视频到底能不能跑通。但你要再去做规模化，就不能纯感性思维了。

你可以去拆解这些爆款。它里面分两部分：一个是钩子，还有你的产品场景。可以把这样的东西复制给不同的content creator去拍。

要么走UGC，让你的KOC去拍；要么你自己内部的运营团队，不停地复制这样内容。比方说我们“2个小时去复习完12个week的PPT”。

Monica

这个钩子一直在用吗？

Bella

这个痛点很明确。“2个小时读完12个week的PPT”，是在讲我们偏文科类的reading的功能，在推场景。

通过这样的一个钩子，一直在复制这种类型的内容。当时复制了很多篇，都能达到同样的效果。

而且这样的内容，它非常直接简单地表达这个产品到底能做什么事，能给我们目标用户带来什么价值，一方面可能容易火，另一方面它的转化率会非常高。比如一个帖子大概一千多个点赞，整体的付费能带来将近100个付费。

Monica

用户的获客成本是多少？

Bella

能够做到的运营获客成本差不多能低到四美金左右，这已经是个非常低的数字了。

主要是因为我们的流量非常精准，而且讲的痛点非常明确，场景也非常高频。所以整体的付费转化率很高，自然流量带来的效果就很大。

Monica

四美金获客成本首次付费最低10美元，首次付费就能打正，这个还是挺厉害的。

观众

是不是有免费版和升级版？

我们之前也试过。但后来觉得这个太硬，我们还是要对学生友好一点，所会给一定的免费额度。

我觉得核心是产品力能够带来更多转化。如果你认为产品足够好，能真正帮助用户解决问题，那也可以让他们先体验产品，让他们真的觉得能带来价值。所以我们也会给一些免费的。

免费的额度是什么样的程度？以时间还是Token？

Bella

不同的场景可能会不太一样。

比方说对于上课，可能会围绕一门课1个小时，至少让你能完完全全听完一堂课，不会需要听40分钟过后发现这东西断了，这非常不友好。

所以也会考虑到用户友好的问题，来设计免费额度。

观众

零成本获客的规模受什么影响？这个是有天花板的吗？比如说在很低的获客成本下，你觉得能做到多少？

Bella

我觉得“零成本”算平台的流行趋势，它会一直变。

最大的瓶颈是你要不停地去track平台上面最近有哪些很火的内容，要非常快去follow趋势。它的卡点在于能很快地去追踪趋势并进行测试。

这里面也有很多自动化的部分。比如使用一些工具，能够帮助你更好地track最近有哪些火的内容。内部团队去做内容，人手是有限的，所以后面还是需要花一些投放费用，比如去找一些KOC博主，变成你的内容共创者，帮你去做矩阵账号内容。要不然就太吃内部团队的运营能力了，人力肯定是有限的。

Monica

拿小红书或者IG来举例，整个工作流是怎么样的？

Bella

首先得根据产品本身去找到相对应的人设。比如他是一个医学生，或者他是学CS的学生，会有不同人设使用产品，场景也会不一样。所以我们会根据这样的人设去选择不同的KOC。我们也会通过一些工具方式，帮助我们筛选更匹配我们想要的博主。

正常就是给博主批量发内容，发email去跟他建立联系。整个过程有点像找KOL。但因为KOC单价更低，所以我们会选择KOC，KOC远比KOL人多，可筛选的池子更多，能找到更多的content creator。后面建立合作，管理这些KOC，让他们去发内容、制作内容，跟他们一起共创，给一些火的钩子和创意参考。

Monica

管理KOC上有什么技巧吗？

Bella

还是挺吃整体运营的。基本上，如果说KOC体量大，像很多AI教育赛道的团队已经做得非常好了，他们会使用一套运营管理系统。比如在Discord社群里面去管理这些KOC。

Monica

你们也用Discord吗？

Bella

看KOC体量到底有多大，5个不到，我觉得都没必要去使用Discord。

Monica

KOC一般怎么找？

Bella

比方说像EasyKOL，是不错找KOL的工具

本质上找KOC和找KOL的逻辑是一样的。只是筛选的时候，找KOL筛选10万粉以上，找KOC是筛选10万粉以下的千粉或几万粉。

一方面选择有潜力的博主，另一方面，如果预算不高，可以按效果付费。我们当时还做了一种方式，找了很多校园大使，也是我们的content creator。

关于校园大使的结算方式，我们会按照每个人有自己的track链接。当时有一个校园大使，一个月做到了一百多个付费。我们有一定的track link可以追踪他的金额，然后跟他进行分成。

Monica

有多少个这样的KOC才能维持一个平台持续增长？

Bella

我个人觉得，能够带来大流量的，一定也就那么几个。

比方说我们之前找了十几个那些校园大使，但真正能够带来大流量、大转化的就那么一两个，效果非常好。所以一开始要多试，留下最好的，让他转化成更核心的成员，让他觉得离产品更近，合作更紧密，加入你们一起。比如我们给那些核心的校园大使“荣誉大使”这样的标签。他们会觉得做这样的事情还是挺骄傲自豪的。

全球化与文化适配策略

Monica

讲到跨语言区域运营，欧美、日韩、韩国。你觉得在文化适配上有一些什么样的技巧吗？

Bella

跨语言学习者跟本地的大学生相比，跨语言学习者更急需翻译。但实际上他们的痛点是相似的。

不管是本土学生还是跨语言学习者，都会看YouTube。我们还有一个功能是YouTube学习，上传YouTube链接，帮你总结。

这个功能我自己都会用，因为我经常看YouTube学习，用这个功能能很快搞明白一个2小时的视频在说什么，做个summary，问一些问题，能让我更好地理解内容。

只是跨语言学习者更有语言障碍问题。但大家其实都要考试，都要做作业，赶deadline的场景是比较类似的，就是有一些东西它可能会不太一样。

Monica

所以你们的用户群体从一开始面向华人的留学生，后来扩张到了其他地区的大学生。

Bella

对。我们一开始做的是小红书跟抖音渠道，上面留学生会比较多。后来做了TikTok跟IG，我们就做了很多国家的矩阵账号，包括北美号、韩语号、日语号，还有一些台湾繁体字号，触达非留学生的人群。

因为TikTok跟IG的人群非常多，全球的人都在用。韩国人很爱刷，你在上面做内容，就能够触达这些韩国人。所以是渠道驱动。

Monica

所以是增长渠道驱动了你们来到不同的目标群体，并不是定好了目标群体，然后去做渠道，是这样吗？

Bella

我们是先做了一些国家的矩阵账号，发现真的可以work。

Monica

打法也是一样的，还是一套内容体系。

你们在整个过程中，产品有跟着做一些什么样的进化吗？随着运营方式和增长。

Bella

产品能够帮助找到更多的发力点。

如果一直只做解题类，很快会发力不上去了，因为这类产品会越来越多。所以要找到新的突破点，这就是为什么当时要找到新的场景功能。

一般来说，我们三个月会出一个大的版本迭代，每个月都会做一些小功能迭代。做这些是为了有更多的突破点，帮助我们获取付费用户。

行业思考与未来展望

Monica

这一类教育和学习的工具，听起来这些功能好像没有什么非常稀缺和特别，假如用GPT或者一些工具都能替代，比如分析文档、解题。

你怎么看这个产品的发展和天花板？

Bella

我觉得AskSia做all-in-one（一站式解决方案） 这件事是make sense的。比方说今天我上课，得用着GPT，再加上用一个会议纪要功能工具，同时使用两个工具，没办法串联在一起。转录之后，还得导入到GPT上面，很麻烦。

做all-in-one的逻辑，是希望能够减少这种磨损。你能花十美金，完整体验学习当中最大的三个痛点。不然用户迁移成本太高了。一会儿用这个产品，一会儿用那个产品，两个产品加起来要花超过50美金。

还不如只花十美金做这三件事。

我觉得用户迁移成本比较高。因为在产品上面，用户有很多历史使用数据，还有上课的录音记录。他要复习的时候，还是最后考试的时候，都需要拿之前的录音帮助复习。

所以他一旦在产品上留下的痕迹越强，迁移成本会更高。包括我们当时有很多用户，放假确实取消订阅了，但到开学时候又想起来，一定要去使用AskSia，又开始回来续费了。可能产品本身留存做得还不错，然后用户自己的迁移成本也比较高，所以整体的用户留存相对来说也会比较高。

观众

有没有核心功能被使用多，其他功能用得不多的情况？

Bella

如果用户一直使用某个功能，比如因为翻译功能进来的，会一直盯着翻译功能。这也是我们当时的一些疑问，怎么让用户知道原来产品上还有一些别的功能，但这是可以优化的。比如一进来的主页面，就能够很快get到这个产品还能做什么。

产品要做足够简单，一上来用户就知道在页面上能做什么事，不能做得很复杂。找不到入口，太复杂了，用户就不太清楚怎么使用别的功能。

Monica

你们的产品跟运营会打架吗？

Bella

我觉得经常会。比方说产品觉得自己做的产品很好。

之前我们做了一个功能，针对上网课的人可以丢一些zoom link进来，帮他上好一门课。但这个功能当时没有达到预期，产品跟运营之间出现了一些battle。但我们后来迅速地上了一个实时的上课转录翻译功能，确实通过这个功能帮我们抓到了更多人群。

我觉得不是说上网课人不够多，而是实时上课的这群人，他们对翻译功能的需求会更多。因为用户真的在上课那个场景上，会更需要一个工具帮助他翻译老师讲的英文或其他语言，这个功能的付费转化率就上来了。

Monica

这种to C的针对性人群的产品，怎么去做好build in public？

Bella

我觉得做to C的产品，更多的是用户心智。比方说我们当时想打造的是，当你学习，你就要想到AskSia。要有这样直接明了的把用户心智植入到潜在用户里面。像原来那种洗脑广告语，比如“送礼就送脑白金”。

我觉得用户心智这个东西本身就是会需要不停地以这种洗脑的方式，让大家有这样的认知：学习上面就要去使用你的产品，把你的场景和产品结合在一起。这会让用户粘性非常深。

Monica

最后请Bella送一句话给大家

Bella

尽早让用户付钱。这样也能更快的去测试你的产品PMF。
