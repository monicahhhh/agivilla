Monica Wang Founder, AGI Villa

In the previous articles, we analyzed Southeast Asia from a country perspective. In this piece, we shift to an “industry perspective.”

The overseas expansion of Chinese companies in Southeast Asia can roughly be divided into three waves:

  • First wave (1990s–2010): State-owned enterprises in energy, mining, and infrastructure

  • Second wave (2011–2020): Manufacturing companies + internet giants

  • Third wave (2021–present): New energy vehicles + batteries + consumer brands + AI

Each wave had different leaders, underlying logic, success stories, and lessons from failure.

The First Wave (1990s–2010): Energy and Infrastructure Led by the National Team

The period from the 1990s to 2010 marked the early stage of China’s “going global” strategy. It was driven by national-level resource security and strategic investment, with state-owned enterprises as the main players — CNPC, Sinopec, CNOOC, PowerChina, CCCC, Chinalco, Sinosteel, CAMCE, and others.

Southeast Asia was one of the core destinations for China’s first wave of overseas expansion — oil and gas, minerals, and power.

Representative Cases

1.CNPC — Natural Gas in Myanmar

From 2004 to 2013, CNPC led the construction of the China–Myanmar oil and gas pipelines — running from Kyaukpyu Port in western Myanmar on the Bay of Bengal, across Myanmar to Ruili in Yunnan, and then onward to Guangxi, Guizhou, and Chongqing. This pipeline is a core part of China’s “land-based energy lifeline.” Today, it transports more than 5 billion cubic meters of natural gas and over 13 million tons of crude oil annually.

2.Sinopec — Indonesia

In 2009, Sinopec acquired partial interests in Indonesia’s Natuna D-Alpha gas field through acquisition, marking one of the earliest major energy investments by a Chinese state-owned enterprise in Indonesia.

3.CNOOC — Indonesia

CNOOC operates multiple offshore oil and gas blocks in Indonesia and is one of the major operators in the Malacca Strait and eastern Indonesian waters.

4.PowerChina — Nam Ou Hydropower Station in Laos

The Nam Ou hydropower project is a “cascade hydropower” project built by PowerChina in Laos. With seven stations and a total installed capacity of 1.27 million kilowatts, it is the largest hydropower project in Laos and a core part of the country’s “Battery of Southeast Asia” strategy.

5.MCC — Indonesia’s Ruiqing Ferronickel Project

MCC Group’s Ruiqing ferronickel project, established in Sulawesi in 2007, was one of the earliest overseas representatives of China’s ferronickel industry and helped trigger the later wave of nickel industrial parks in Indonesia.

6.China Railway — Jakarta–Bandung High-Speed Railway in Indonesia

The Jakarta–Bandung High-Speed Railway is the first standard-gauge high-speed railway built by China overseas — 142 kilometers long, with a top speed of 350 km/h, officially opened in October 2023. CRRC supplied the high-speed trains, while China Railway International and China Railway Construction International undertook the construction. It is a landmark Chinese export to Indonesia.

Characteristics and Challenges of the First Wave

Characteristics:

State credit backing, long-term capital, high-level government relationships with host countries, and alignment with local resource endowments.

Challenges:

  • Early projects relied heavily on “government relationships” and had insufficient understanding of local laws, labor rules, and environmental standards.

  • A lack of localized operational experience — many projects employed large numbers of Chinese workers without developing local talent.

  • Weak exchange-rate risk management — depreciation of the Indonesian rupiah, Myanmar kyat, and Vietnamese dong repeatedly caused major losses for Chinese companies.

  • Lagging ESG standards — environmental protests and labor accidents.

The Second Wave (2011–2020): Manufacturing + Internet Giants

2011 was a turning point for Chinese manufacturing expansion into Southeast Asia. Rapidly rising labor costs in China, RMB appreciation, and pressure from industrial upgrading pushed Chinese manufacturers to “actively relocate” production overseas. At the same time, Chinese internet companies began to globalize after the domestic market became increasingly saturated.

Representative Manufacturing Cases

7.Hongdou Group — Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone in Cambodia

Wuxi-based Hongdou Group began building the Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone in Cambodia in 2008. It was one of the earliest examples of a Chinese private enterprise developing an overseas industrial park. Today, the zone hosts around 200 companies and 35,000 employees.

8.Midea, Haier, TCL — Vietnam and Thailand

China’s three major home appliance giants began building manufacturing bases in Vietnam and Thailand in the 2010s:

  • Midea Vietnam factories in Bac Ninh and Binh Duong: annual capacity of 8 million air conditioners and 2 million washing machines.

  • Haier Thailand factory in Chonburi: a refrigerator production base for Southeast Asia.

  • TCL Vietnam factory: producing televisions and air conditioners for export to Europe and the United States.

9.Luxshare Precision and GoerTek — Northern Vietnam

Two major Chinese suppliers in Apple’s supply chain — Luxshare Precision and GoerTek — began building large factories in northern Vietnam, including Bac Giang and Vinh Phuc, from 2017 onward. They became key assembly bases in Vietnam for iPhones, AirPods, and Apple Watch. Luxshare has more than 100,000 employees in Vietnam and is one of the largest Chinese employers in the country.

10.Shenzhou International and Bosideng — Apparel in Southeast Asia

Shenzhou International, China’s leading apparel manufacturer and the largest supplier to Nike, Adidas, and Uniqlo, has built large textile bases in Cambodia and Vietnam. Bosideng has established down jacket production bases in Myanmar and Bangladesh.

11.Shandong Ruyi and Huafu Fashion — Cambodia and Vietnam

Chinese textile, dyeing, and printing giants have built integrated cotton yarn, dyeing, and garment production bases in Cambodia and Vietnam.

12.Robam and Hisense — Southeast Asia

Robam has established kitchen appliance production bases in Thailand and Indonesia. Hisense has built Southeast Asia’s largest color TV production line in Thailand.

Representative Internet Cases

13.Alibaba— Lazada

In 2016, Alibaba invested US$1 billion in Lazada, one of Southeast Asia’s largest e-commerce platforms. In 2017, Alibaba increased its stake to take control, and continued investing in 2022. Lazada covers six countries — Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, the Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam — and is the core of Alibaba’s Southeast Asia strategy.

14.Tencent System — Sea Group / Shopee

Tencent once held around 23% of Singapore-based Sea Group, which owns Garena games, Shopee e-commerce, and SeaMoney financial services, before gradually reducing its stake. Shopee is Southeast Asia’s largest e-commerce platform, with more than 450 million monthly active users and around US$110 billion in GMV in 2025.

15.JD.com — Tiki / J&T Express

JD.com invested in Vietnam’s Tiki, though it later exited. At the same time, JD Logistics has worked closely with local logistics players such as JNE. J&T Express is a courier company linked to the BBK–OPPO/vivo ecosystem. It started in Indonesia and listed in Hong Kong in 2023.

16.ByteDance — TikTok in Southeast Asia

TikTok began aggressively expanding into Southeast Asia in 2018. Today, it has more than 150 million monthly active users in Indonesia, 65 million in Vietnam, 55 million in Thailand, 53 million in the Philippines, and 29 million in Malaysia — totaling more than 300 million monthly active users. Southeast Asia is TikTok’s largest non-U.S. regional market globally.

17.Meituan — Food Delivery Exploration

Meituan began studying the Southeast Asian market in 2019, but progress has been limited because Grab, Gojek, Foodpanda, and ShopeeFood had already formed a stable market structure.

18.OPPO / vivo / Xiaomi / Transsion — Smartphones

Chinese smartphone brands began entering Southeast Asia aggressively from 2014 onward:

  • OPPO: established assembly factories in Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, and Indonesia; its Southeast Asia market share was around 22% in 2025.

  • vivo: Southeast Asia market share of around 18%.

  • Xiaomi: Southeast Asia market share of around 16%.

  • Transsion: No.1 in Africa, but relatively weaker in Southeast Asia.

Chinese smartphone brands together account for more than 60% of Southeast Asia’s smartphone market — one of the most important achievements of China’s internet + hardware globalization.

Characteristics and Challenges of the Second Wave

Characteristics:

  • Responding to the impact of the U.S.–China trade war.

  • Southeast Asia’s medium-level labor costs, roughly 50–70% of China’s.

  • Tariff advantages brought by the ASEAN Free Trade Area and RCEP.

  • The rapid rise of Southeast Asia’s consumer market — young population, urbanization, and smartphone penetration.

Challenges:

  • Insufficient localization depth — many manufacturers simply moved production lines to Southeast Asia, but lacked operational depth in cultivating local managers, processes, and supply chains.

  • Underestimating labor laws and union power — labor protests in Vietnam and Indonesia repeatedly led to factory shutdowns.

  • Weak brand power — except for smartphones, Chinese brands in Southeast Asia’s consumer goods market, including home appliances, automobiles, and food, still lag behind Japanese and Korean brands in premium positioning.

The Third Wave (2021–Present): New Energy + AI + Global Brands

2021 marked a new turning point for Chinese companies expanding into Southeast Asia. Several key changes took place that year:

  • The U.S.–China trade war entered a stage of “industrial decoupling,” with increasingly strict restrictions in semiconductors, AI, and biomedicine.

  • China’s new energy supply chain became globally leading — batteries, photovoltaics, and electric vehicles were already facing overcapacity at home and had to expand overseas.

  • Southeast Asia’s market infrastructure had matured — payments, logistics, e-commerce, and consumer mindsets were already in place.

  • The Belt and Road Initiative entered its second decade — shifting from infrastructure-first to a three-wheel drive model of “infrastructure + industry + digital.”

The core feature of the third wave is the shift from “exporting capacity” to “exporting systems” — Chinese companies are no longer just moving factories overseas; they are moving entire supply chains, brands, technologies, and ecosystems into Southeast Asia.

Representative New Energy Cases

19.BYD — Southeast Asia’s EV King

BYD has accelerated its Southeast Asia expansion since 2022:

  • Rayong factory in Thailand: announced in 2022, officially started production in July 2024, and expanded annual capacity to around 200,000 vehicles in 2025. It is BYD’s first overseas complete vehicle factory.

  • Indonesia factory: announced a US$1.3 billion investment in 2024, began construction in 2025, and is expected to start mass production in 2026.

  • Vietnam, Malaysia, and Cambodia: full sales network coverage.

  • In 2025, BYD’s share of Thailand’s EV market exceeded 40%, while it grew rapidly in Singapore, Malaysia, and the Philippines.

20.Great Wall Motor — Thailand and Malaysia

Great Wall Motor acquired General Motors’ Rayong factory in Thailand in 2021, making it the largest Southeast Asian base for Chinese SUVs. Ora, Tank, and Haval are sold across Southeast Asia.

21.SAIC-GM-Wuling — Indonesia

Wuling Air EV, the Indonesian version, was launched in 2022 at a price of only around 185 million Indonesian rupiah, or about US$8,000 and up. It became the top-selling EV in Indonesia, with a single-model market share of more than 55% in 2025.

22.Chery — Multiple Southeast Asian Countries

Since 2022, Chery has aggressively opened stores in Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, and the Philippines. In 2025, Chery’s EV sales in Malaysia continued to exceed BYD’s.

23.Geely — Proton in Malaysia

Geely acquired a 49.9% stake in Malaysia’s national car brand Proton in 2017. Through Proton, Geely has sold models such as Boyue, Lynk & Co, and Zeekr into Southeast Asia, making it a classic example of a Chinese brand “going global through a local shell.”

24.CATL — Indonesian Nickel Industrial Park

In 2022, CATL formed the CBL project with Indonesian state-owned company Antam and Lygend Resources — investing US$6 billion to build a full-chain battery base in North Maluku, covering nickel smelting, battery precursors, cathode materials, and battery cells. This is the largest overseas investment by China’s battery supply chain.

25.Tsingshan Holding Group — Sulawesi, Indonesia

Tsingshan’s IMIP, the Indonesia Morowali Industrial Park, and IWIP, the Indonesia Weda Bay Industrial Park, have accumulated investments of more than US$20 billion and form the world’s largest stainless steel and ferronickel production base. From there, the chain extends downstream into battery precursors, cathode materials, and battery cells. Chinese capital accounts for more than 90% of Indonesia’s nickel industrial chain.

26.Huayou Cobalt, Lygend Resources, Weiming Environment — Indonesia and the Philippines

China’s “three nickel giants” have built large nickel smelting and battery precursor factories in Sulawesi and Maluku in Indonesia, as well as in the Philippines.

27.LONGi, JA Solar, Canadian Solar, Trina Solar — Vietnam, Malaysia, and Thailand

The Southeast Asian capacity of China’s “four photovoltaic leaders”:

  • Vietnam: around 30GW of solar panel capacity.

  • Malaysia: around 15GW, covering the full supply chain of wafers, cells, and modules.

  • Thailand: around 10GW.

In 2024, the United States launched “anti-circumvention” investigations into solar products imported from Vietnam, Malaysia, Thailand, and Cambodia. Starting in 2025, high tariffs began to be imposed, with some product rates exceeding 250%, creating a major shock to the Southeast Asian layout of Chinese photovoltaic companies.

Representative Internet and Consumer Brand Cases

28.Shein — Localized Supply Chain in Southeast Asia

Since 2022, Shein has built local warehouses and supply chains in Southeast Asia, including Thailand, Indonesia, and Vietnam. In 2025, Shein’s Southeast Asia revenue exceeded US$7.5 billion. Shein has moved its headquarters to Singapore.

29.Temu, PDD’s Overseas Version — Southeast Asia

Temu entered Southeast Asia in 2024 — entering the Philippines, Malaysia, and Thailand in August, and Vietnam in November. By the end of 2025, Temu had more than 20 million monthly active users in the Philippines and more than 15 million in Malaysia.

30.TikTok Shop — Southeast Asia’s New E-commerce King

TikTok Shop launched in Indonesia in 2022 and became Southeast Asia’s third-largest e-commerce platform by 2024, behind only Shopee and Lazada. In 2025, its Southeast Asia GMV was around US$43 billion. However, Indonesia banned TikTok Shop in September 2023. TikTok re-entered Indonesia’s e-commerce market by acquiring Tokopedia, Indonesia’s largest local e-commerce platform, in a deal valued at around US$1.5 billion.

31.J&T Express — Across Southeast Asia

J&T Express, linked to the BBK ecosystem and backed by OPPO/vivo founder Duan Yongping, started in Indonesia and has become one of Southeast Asia’s largest courier companies. In 2025, its daily order volume in Southeast Asia exceeded 25 million. It listed in Hong Kong in 2023.

32.Cainiao, JD Logistics, SF Express — Cross-Border Logistics in Southeast Asia

Cainiao has built “Southeast Asia distribution centers” in Malaysia and Thailand. JD Logistics has established overseas warehouses in Singapore and Malaysia. SF Express acquired Asian logistics giant Kerry Logistics, covering multiple Southeast Asian countries.

33.miHoYo, Lilith Games, NetEase — Gaming Going Global

Chinese game companies have achieved strong results in Southeast Asia: Genshin Impact has more than 15 million monthly active users in Southeast Asia; Lilith’s AFK Arena and Rise of Kingdoms have long ranked near the top in Southeast Asia; NetEase’s Eggy Party and Naraka: Bladepoint have grown rapidly in the region.

34.Mixue, Luckin Coffee, Cotti Coffee — F&B Going Global

Chinese tea drink and coffee brands have entered Southeast Asia aggressively since 2022:

  • Mixue: more than 4,000 stores across Indonesia, Vietnam, Malaysia, Thailand, and the Philippines, making it the Chinese F&B brand with the largest number of stores in Southeast Asia.

  • Luckin Coffee: entered Singapore, Malaysia, and Indonesia from 2023 onward.

  • Cotti Coffee: racing against Luckin to enter Southeast Asia.

  • Heytea, Chagee, and ChaPanda: opened stores in Singapore and Kuala Lumpur.

35.Florasis, Perfect Diary, Judydoll — Beauty Brands Going Global

Chinese emerging beauty brands focused heavily on Southeast Asia from 2023 to 2025. Florasis sells through TikTok Shop and Shopee in Malaysia, Indonesia, and Thailand; Perfect Diary entered offline retail channels in Singapore.

36.DJI — Drones

DJI is the absolute leader in consumer drones in Southeast Asia, with a market share of more than 70%.

Characteristics and Challenges of the Third Wave

Characteristics:

  • Complete supply-chain coordination — from raw materials to batteries, vehicles, charging piles, and financial services.

  • Triple output of brand + technology + localization — not just low prices.

  • Capturing the window of consumption upgrading — Southeast Asian consumers no longer see Chinese brands as synonymous with “cheap.”

  • Ecosystem playbook — integration of TikTok + Shopee + logistics + payments + influencers.

Unresolved Challenges:

  • Uncertainty around U.S. “anti-circumvention” tariffs.

  • Southeast Asian countries’ alertness toward the “overconcentration” of Chinese capital.

  • International standards for ESG, labor rights, and environmental protection.

  • The depth of talent localization.

Five Tracks

The most promising directions in the third wave can be summarized into five tracks:

Track 1: New Energy (Batteries + EVs + Photovoltaics)

  • Market size: Southeast Asia’s EV market is expected to reach US$300 billion by 2030.

  • Core countries: Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Vietnam.

  • Core players: BYD, CATL, Tsingshan, Great Wall Motor, Chery, Wuling, CATL, and LONGi.

  • Core opportunities: local assembly + local batteries + charging networks + consumer finance + used-car recycling.

Track 2: E-commerce and Ecosystems

  • Market size: Southeast Asia’s e-commerce GMV was around US$125 billion in 2025 and is expected to reach US$400 billion by 2030.

  • Core players: Shopee, Tencent-backed; Lazada, Alibaba-backed; TikTok Shop, ByteDance-backed; Temu, PDD-backed; and Shein.

  • Core opportunities: livestream e-commerce + short video + influencer marketing + local warehouses + overseas supply chains + cross-border payments.

Track 3: Digital Content and Gaming

  • Market size: Southeast Asia’s digital entertainment market is around US$20 billion per year.

  • Core players: TikTok, miHoYo, NetEase, Lilith Games, and Tencent Games.

  • Core opportunities: localized game development + overseas publishing + IP partnerships + influencer economy.

Track 4: AI and Data Centers (see the next article for details)

Track 5: Consumer Brands (Tea Drinks, F&B, Beauty, Home Appliances, Consumer Electronics)

  • Core players: Mixue, Luckin Coffee, Haidilao, Florasis, Xiaomi, Haier, TCL, and Midea.

  • Core opportunities: the consumption-upgrading window of Southeast Asia’s 690 million people; China’s advantage in “value for money + innovation.”

The “Five Iron Rules” for Expanding into Southeast Asia

The experience and lessons from the three waves of Chinese companies expanding into Southeast Asia can be summarized into five iron rules:

Iron Rule 1: Local Partners Are Indispensable

Every Southeast Asian country has powerful local conglomerates — Indonesia’s Sinar Mas, Lippo, and Salim; the Philippines’ SM, Ayala, and JG Summit; Thailand’s CP, TCC, and Central; Malaysia’s Genting, Berjaya, and Sime Darby; and Singapore’s Temasek-linked ecosystem. Without local partners, foreign companies find it difficult to truly land. BYD’s joint venture with CP in Thailand, TCL’s partnership with VietHan in Vietnam, and Huawei’s cooperation with PT Telkom in Indonesia are all useful references.

Iron Rule 2: Do Not Copy Chinese Speed

Southeast Asia’s legal, regulatory, labor, land, and tax processes are much slower than China’s. Many Chinese companies’ “aggressive playbook,” such as burning money to grab market share, can trigger caution and backlash from local governments. Indonesia’s ban on TikTok Shop, the Philippines’ ban on POGO, and Malaysia’s requirement that Country Garden’s Forest City adjust its target buyers all reflect the cost of ignoring this rule.

Iron Rule 3: Understand the “Composite Matrix” of Diverse Cultures

Southeast Asia is not one market; it is ten different markets. Indonesia is Muslim-majority, Thailand is Buddhist, the Philippines is Catholic, Singapore is multicultural, and Vietnam has deep Confucian cultural roots. Products, marketing, and HR all require deep localization. One of the keys to Haidilao and Mixue’s success in Southeast Asia has been deep menu localization, including halal versions, vegetarian options, and adjusted spice levels.

Iron Rule 4: Compliance First; Gray Businesses Will Die

Southeast Asia’s “gray businesses” are already being jointly cracked down on by governments across the region and the Chinese government. Companies that rely on “gray zones” will eventually suffer major blows. Compliance, tax transparency, and ESG standards are now hard thresholds for Southeast Asia.

Iron Rule 5: Long-Termism + Patient Capital

Southeast Asia is not a place to make quick money. Temasek’s success in Singapore took 50 years; Lee Kum Kee’s roots in Malaysia took 80 years; CP Group’s cooperation with China has lasted 40 years. Only Chinese companies willing to spend 10 years taking root in Southeast Asia have the chance to become the next generation of global giants.

The “Chinese Paradigm” for Southeast Asia Expansion

After more than three decades of practice, Chinese companies have developed a unique “going global paradigm” in Southeast Asia:

  • The integration of “capacity + brand + localization.”

  • The multilateral model of “open ecosystems + partners.”

  • A “regional-level layout” covering all 10 ASEAN countries.

  • A full industrial chain spanning “infrastructure + manufacturing + internet + finance + consumption.”

前面几篇我们从国家视角拆解了东南亚，这一篇我们切换到”产业视角”。

中国企业在东南亚的出海，时间线大致可以分为三代浪潮：

·第一代（1990年代-2010年）：能源、矿业、基建国企

·第二代（2011-2020年）：制造业+互联网巨头

·第三代（2021年至今）：新能源汽车+电池+消费品牌+AI

每一代浪潮的领头者、背后逻辑、成功案例和失败教训都不相同。

第一代浪潮（1990s-2010）：国家队的能源与基建

1990年代到2010年是中国”走出去”战略的初期，是国家层面的资源安全和战略投资，主角是国企——中石油、中石化、中海油、中电建、中交建、中铝、中钢、中工等。

东南亚是中国第一波出海的核心目的地之一——油气、矿产、电力。

代表案例

1.中石油—缅甸天然气

2004-2013年，中石油主导建设了中缅油气管道——从缅甸西部皎漂港（孟加拉湾）穿越缅甸全境到中国云南瑞丽，再到广西、贵州、重庆。这条管道是中国”陆上能源生命线”的核心，目前每年运输天然气超过50亿立方米、原油超过1300万吨。

2.中石化—印尼

中石化2009年通过收购获得印尼纳吐纳D-Alpha气田部分权益，是中国国企在印尼最早的能源大手笔。

3.中海油—印尼CNOOC

中海油在印尼有多个海上油气作业区，是马六甲海峡及印尼东部海域的主力作业方。

4.中电建—老挝南欧江水电站

南欧江水电站是中国电建集团在老挝建设的”梯级水电”项目——七级电站总装机容量127万千瓦，是老挝最大的水电项目，也是”东南亚电池”战略的核心。

5.中冶—印尼瑞青镍铁

中冶集团2007年在苏拉威西建立的瑞青镍铁项目，是中国镍铁产业海外的早期代表，开启了后来印尼镍工业园的浪潮。

6.中铁—印尼雅万高铁

雅万高铁是中国海外建成的第一条标准轨高铁——142公里、最高时速350公里，2023年10月通车。中国中车提供高速动车组，中铁国际、中铁建国际承建，是中国对印尼的标志性出口。

第一代的特点和挑战

特点：

国家信用背书、长期资金、与所在国政府高层关系、资源禀赋匹配。

挑战：

·早期项目依赖”政府关系”，对当地法律、劳工、环境规范理解不足。

·缺乏本地化运营经验——很多项目雇佣大量中国劳工，没有培养本地人才。

·汇率风险管理薄弱——印尼盾、缅元、越南盾的贬值多次让中资企业巨亏。

·ESG标准滞后——环保抗议、劳工事故。

第二代浪潮（2011-2020）：制造业+互联网巨头

2011年是中国制造业出海东南亚的”拐点”——中国劳动力成本快速上升、人民币升值、产业升级压力让中国制造业开始”主动外迁”。同时，中国互联网企业在国内市场饱和后开始全球化。

制造业代表案例

7.红豆集团—柬埔寨西哈努克港特区

无锡红豆集团2008年开始在柬埔寨西哈努克港建设”经济特区”——是中国民企海外建园区的早期代表。今天，西港特区有约200家入驻企业、3.5万员工。

8.美的、海尔、TCL—越南、泰国

中国家电三巨头从2010年代开始在越南、泰国建立家电制造基地：

·美的越南工厂（北宁、平阳）：年产能空调800万台、洗衣机200万台。

·海尔泰国工厂（春武里）：东南亚冰箱生产基地。

·TCL越南工厂：电视、空调出口欧美。

9.立讯精密、歌尔股份—越南北部

苹果产业链的两大中国供应商——立讯精密和歌尔股份——从2017年开始在越南北部（北江、永福）建立大型工厂，是iPhone、AirPods、Apple Watch的越南组装核心。立讯精密在越南员工超过10万，是越南最大的中资雇主之一。

10.申洲国际、波司登—东南亚服装

中国服装制造龙头申洲国际（耐克、阿迪、优衣库的最大供应商）在柬埔寨、越南建立大型纺织基地。波司登在缅甸、孟加拉建立羽绒服生产基地。

11.山东如意、华孚色纺—柬埔寨、越南

中国纺织印染巨头在柬埔寨、越南建立棉纱、印染、服装一条龙基地。

12.老板电器、海信—东南亚

老板电器在泰国、印尼建立厨电生产基地。海信在泰国建立东南亚最大彩电生产线。

互联网代表案例

13.阿里巴巴—Lazada

2016年，阿里巴巴以10亿美元入股Lazada（东南亚最大电商平台之一），2017年增持至控股，2022年继续增资。Lazada覆盖印尼、马来西亚、新加坡、菲律宾、泰国、越南六国——是阿里东南亚战略的核心。

14.腾讯系—Sea Group / Shopee

腾讯曾持有新加坡Sea Group（旗下Garena游戏+Shopee电商+SeaMoney金融）约23%股份（后逐步减持）。Shopee是东南亚最大电商，月活超过4.5亿，2025年GMV约1100亿美元。

15.京东—Tiki / J&T Express

京东在越南投资Tiki（已退出）；同时京东物流深度合作JNE等本地物流。J&T Express（极兔）是步步高—OPPO/vivo系的快递公司，从印尼起家，2023年香港上市。

16.字节跳动—TikTok东南亚

TikTok从2018年开始大举进军东南亚，目前印尼月活超过1.5亿、越南6500万、泰国5500万、菲律宾5300万、马来西亚2900万——合计超过3亿月活。东南亚是TikTok全球最大的非美区域市场。

17.美团—外卖（探索阶段）

美团2019年开始研究东南亚市场，但由于Grab、Gojek、Foodpanda、ShopeeFood已经形成稳定格局，进展有限。

18.OPPO/vivo/小米/传音—智能手机

中国手机品牌2014年起大举进入东南亚：

·OPPO：在印尼、越南、泰国、印尼建立组装厂；2025年东南亚市场份额约22%。

·vivo：东南亚市场份额约18%。

·小米：东南亚市场份额约16%。

·传音（Transsion）：在非洲第一，但在东南亚表现一般。

中国手机合计占东南亚智能手机市场份额超过60%——是中国互联网+硬件出海最重要的成就之一。

第二代的特点和挑战

特点：

·应对中美贸易战的影响。

·东南亚的中等劳动力成本（约中国50-70%）。

·东盟自贸区+RCEP带来的关税优惠。

·东南亚消费市场的快速崛起——年轻、城镇化、智能手机普及。

挑战：

·本地化深度不足——很多制造企业只把生产线搬到东南亚，对于当地管理人员的培养、工艺和供应链建设缺少运营深度。

·对劳工法律和工会力量低估——越南、印尼的劳工抗议多次导致工厂停工。

·品牌力不足——除手机外，中国品牌在东南亚消费品市场（家电、汽车、食品）的”高端化”仍弱于日韩。

第三代浪潮（2021至今）：新能源+AI+全球品牌

2021年，是中国出海东南亚的新拐点。这一年的几个关键变化：

·中美贸易战进入”产业脱钩”阶段——半导体、AI、生物医药领域的限制日趋严格。

·中国新能源产业链全球领先——电池、光伏、电动车在中国国内已经过剩，必须出海。

·东南亚市场基础设施已经成熟——支付、物流、电商、消费心智都已建立。

·“一带一路”进入第二个十年——从基建为主转向”基建+产业+数字”三轮驱动。

第三代浪潮的核心特征：从”输出产能”到”输出体系”——中国企业不再只是把工厂搬过去，而是把整个产业链、品牌、技术、生态都搬到东南亚。

新能源代表案例

19.比亚迪—东南亚电动车王

比亚迪从2022年起加速东南亚布局：

·泰国罗勇府工厂：2022年宣布投资，2024年7月正式投产，2025年年产能扩至约20万辆，是比亚迪海外第一个整车工厂。

·印尼工厂：2024年宣布投资13亿美元，2025年开工建设，2026年量产。

·越南、马来西亚、柬埔寨：销售网络全覆盖。

·2025年，比亚迪在泰国电动车市场份额超过40%；在新加坡、马来西亚、菲律宾增长迅速。

20.长城汽车—泰国、马来西亚

长城汽车2021年收购通用泰国罗勇工厂，是中国SUV在东南亚的最大基地。Ora、Tank、Haval品牌在东南亚销售。

21.上汽通用五菱—印尼

五菱Air EV（印尼版本）2022年发布，价格仅约1.85亿印尼盾（约8000美元起），在印尼电动车销量第一——单车型2025年市场份额超过55%。

22.奇瑞—东南亚多国

奇瑞2022年起在马来西亚、印尼、泰国、越南、菲律宾大举开店——2025年奇瑞在马来西亚电动车销量持续超过比亚迪。

23.吉利—Proton（马来西亚）

吉利2017年收购马来西亚国民车Proton 49.9%股权——通过Proton把吉利博越、领克、极氪销售到东南亚，是中国品牌”借壳出海”的典范。

24.宁德时代—印尼镍工业园

宁德时代2022年与印尼国企Antam和洛克锂业合资成立CBL项目——投资60亿美元在北马鲁古建设全产业链电池基地（镍冶炼+电池前驱体+正极+电芯）。这是中国电池产业链海外最大投资。

25.青山控股—印尼苏拉威西

青山控股的IMIP（印尼Morowali工业园）+IWIP（印尼Weda Bay工业园）：累计投资超过200亿美元，是全球最大不锈钢和镍铁生产基地。从这里，下游延伸到电池前驱体、电池正极、电芯。印尼镍工业链中国资本占比超过90%。

26.华友钴业、力勤资源、伟明环保—印尼、菲律宾

中国”镍三巨头”在印尼苏拉威西、马鲁古以及菲律宾建立大型镍冶炼+电池前驱体工厂。

27.隆基绿能、晶澳科技、阿特斯、天合光能—越南、马来西亚、泰国

中国太阳能”光伏四剑客”在东南亚的产能：

·越南：约30GW太阳能板产能。

·马来西亚：约15GW，包括硅片、电池、组件全产业链。

·泰国：约10GW。

2024年，美国对从越南、马来西亚、泰国、柬埔寨进口的太阳能产品发起”反规避”调查，2025年起开始征收高额关税（部分产品税率超过250%），给中国光伏的东南亚布局带来重大冲击。

互联网与消费品牌代表案例

28.Shein—东南亚本土化供应链

Shein 2022年起在东南亚（泰国、印尼、越南）建立本地仓和供应链，2025年东南亚营收超过75亿美元。Shein总部已迁至新加坡。

29.Temu（拼多多海外版）—东南亚

Temu 2024年起进入东南亚——8月进菲律宾、马来西亚、泰国，11月进越南。截至2025年底，Temu在菲律宾月活超过2000万、在马来西亚超过1500万。

30.TikTok Shop—东南亚电商新王

TikTok Shop 2022年在印尼上线，到2024年成为东南亚第三大电商平台（仅次于Shopee、Lazada）。2025年东南亚GMV约430亿美元。但印尼曾在2023年9月禁止TikTok Shop，TikTok通过收购Tokopedia（印尼最大本土电商，价值约15亿美元）的方式重新进入印尼电商市场。

31.极兔速递（J&T Express）—全东南亚

J&T Express（步步高系，OPPO/vivo创始人段永平投资）从印尼起家，已成为东南亚最大快递公司之一——2025年东南亚日单量超过2500万。2023年香港上市。

32.菜鸟、京东物流、顺丰速运—东南亚跨境物流

菜鸟在马来西亚、泰国建立”东南亚配送中心”；京东物流在新加坡、马来西亚建立海外仓；顺丰收购了亚洲物流巨头嘉里物流（Kerry Logistics），覆盖东南亚多国。

33.米哈游、莉莉丝、网易—游戏出海

中国游戏公司在东南亚的成绩单：《原神》在东南亚月活超过1500万；莉莉丝《剑与远征》、《万国觉醒》在东南亚长期榜单前列；网易《蛋仔派对》、《永劫无间》在东南亚增长迅速。

34.蜜雪冰城、瑞幸咖啡、库迪咖啡—餐饮出海

中国茶饮和咖啡品牌从2022年起大举进入东南亚：

·蜜雪冰城：在印尼、越南、马来西亚、泰国、菲律宾合计开店超过4000家，是东南亚门店最多的中国餐饮品牌。

·瑞幸咖啡：2023年起进入新加坡、马来西亚、印尼。

·库迪咖啡：与瑞幸赛跑进入东南亚。

·喜茶、霸王茶姬、茶百道：在新加坡、吉隆坡开店。

35.花西子、完美日记、橘朵—美妆出海

中国新锐美妆品牌2023-2025年集中发力东南亚——花西子在马来西亚、印尼、泰国通过TikTok Shop和Shopee销售；完美日记进入新加坡线下渠道。

36.大疆—无人机

大疆在东南亚是消费级无人机的绝对领导者——市场份额超过70%。

第三代的特点和挑战

特点：

·完整的产业链协同——从原材料到电池到整车到充电桩到金融服务。

·品牌+技术+本地化的三重输出——不只是低价。

·把握消费升级窗口——东南亚消费者看中国品牌已经不再是”廉价的代名词”。

·生态打法——TikTok+Shopee+物流+支付+网红一体化。

未解的挑战：

·美国”反规避”关税的不确定性。

·东南亚各国对中国资本的”过度集中”警觉。

·ESG、劳工权益、环境保护的国际标准。

·人才本地化的深度。

五个赛道

归纳第三代浪潮中最有前景的方向，可以形成五个赛道：

赛道1：新能源（电池+电动车+光伏）

·市场规模：东南亚电动车市场预计2030年达到3000亿美元。

·核心国家：泰国、印尼、马来西亚、越南。

·核心玩家：比亚迪、宁德时代、青山、长城、奇瑞、五菱、宁德时代、隆基。

·核心机会：本地组装+本地电池+充电网络+消费金融+二手车回收。

赛道2：电商与生态

·市场规模：东南亚电商GMV 2025年约1250亿美元，2030年预计4000亿美元。

·核心玩家：Shopee（腾讯系）、Lazada（阿里系）、TikTok Shop（字节系）、Temu（拼多多系）、Shein。

·核心机会：直播电商+短视频+网红营销+本地仓+海外供应链+跨境支付。

赛道3：数字内容与游戏

·市场规模：东南亚数字娱乐市场约200亿美元/年。

·核心玩家：TikTok、米哈游、网易、莉莉丝、腾讯游戏。

·核心机会：本地化游戏研发+海外发行+IP合作+网红经济。

赛道4：AI与数据中心（详见下一篇）

赛道5：消费品牌（茶饮、餐饮、美妆、家电、消费电子）

·核心玩家：蜜雪冰城、瑞幸、海底捞、花西子、小米、海尔、TCL、美的。

·核心机会：东南亚6.9亿人口的消费升级窗口；中国”性价比+创新”优势。

出海东南亚的”五条铁律”

中国企业出海东南亚三代浪潮的经验和教训，可以汇总为五条铁律：

铁律1：本地化合伙人不可或缺

东南亚每个国家都有强大的本地财阀——印尼的金光、力宝、三林；菲律宾的SM、Ayala、JG Summit；泰国的CP、TCC、Central；马来西亚的Genting、Berjaya、Sime Darby；新加坡的淡马锡系。没有本地合伙人，外资企业很难真正落地。比亚迪在泰国与CP合资、TCL在越南与VietHan合作、华为在印尼与PT Telkom合作——都值得参考。

铁律2：不要复制中国式速度

东南亚的法律、监管、劳工、土地、税务流程比中国慢得多。许多中国企业的”激进打法”（如疯狂砸钱抢市场份额）会引发当地政府警惕和反弹——印尼禁TikTok Shop、菲律宾禁POGO、马来西亚要求碧桂园森林城市改变销售对象，都是这条铁律的代价。

铁律3：理解多元文化的”复合矩阵”

东南亚不是一个市场，是10个不同市场。印尼穆斯林、泰国佛教徒、菲律宾天主教徒、新加坡多元、越南儒家文化——产品、营销、HR都要深度本地化。海底捞、蜜雪冰城在东南亚成功的关键之一就是菜单深度本地化（清真版、素食版、辣度调整）。

铁律4：合规优先，灰色业务必死

东南亚的”灰色业务”，已经被各国政府和中国政府联合打击。依赖”灰色地带”的企业最终都会受到重创。合规、税务透明、ESG标准已经是东南亚的硬门槛。

铁律5：长期主义+耐心资本

东南亚不是”赚快钱”的地方。新加坡淡马锡的成功是50年；李锦记在马来西亚的扎根是80年；正大集团在中国的合作是40年。愿意花10年时间在东南亚扎根的中国企业，才有机会成为下一代的全球巨头。

东南亚出海的”中国范式”

经过三十多年的实践，中国企业在东南亚已经形成了独特的”出海范式”：

·”产能+品牌+本地化”的融合。

·”开放生态+合作伙伴”的多边。

·覆盖东盟10国的”区域级布局”。

·”基建+制造+互联网+金融+消费”的全产业链。
