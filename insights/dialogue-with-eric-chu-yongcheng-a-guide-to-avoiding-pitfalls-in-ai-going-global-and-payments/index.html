Key Differences Between PSP and MOR

Monica

Today, we've invited Eric Chu Yongcheng, CMO of Waffo, as our guest.

Waffo is a rapidly developing payment solutions company, and Eric has over a decade of experience in cross-border payments, having worked at Tencent and Ant Group, and participated in the early-stage development of two cross-border payment startups, gaining rich practical experience from 0 to 1. Next, I'd like to invite Eric to provide further introduction.

Eric

Thank you, Monica, and greetings to everyone at AGI Villa and Monica's Notes.

Over the past decade, I've focused on the cross-border payment sector. Previously, my core focus was on cross-border e-commerce, but now our business scope has expanded to include all categories of online digital businesses, covering scenarios such as digital entertainment going global, providing integrated payment + monetization solutions for clients.

In addition to my roles at Tencent and Ant Group, I also participated in building two cross-border payment startups from 0 to 1, ultimately achieving tens of billions of dollars in transaction volume, accumulating extensive practical experience in cross-border payments.

Let me also introduce Waffo: The company was founded in 2023, and its core team members come from Ant International, foreign banks, and major internet companies, with founding members generally having over ten years of experience in cross-border payments. Currently, we serve several major domestic game companies expanding overseas, and since 2025, we have also begun to focus on the AI and developer overseas expansion sectors, accumulating a number of clients in related fields.

During our service, I've noticed that many developers focus their main efforts on growth, product refinement, and traffic acquisition, while payment, as a relatively complex field, often lacks professional information channels, making it easy to fall into some basic but significant pitfalls. Today, I will address the 9 major core "pitfalls" in cross-border payments, combining the high-frequency issues encountered by everyone, and I welcome questions and discussions at any time.

Monica

Thank you, Eric, for your insights. Today, we won't focus on superficial questions like "which PSP is cheaper," but rather delve into the "black hole area" of cross-border payments: Why do some people achieve over 90% payment success rates for the same product, while others get stuck at 60%-70%? Many teams initially blame product or traffic, but the real issue lies in the payment end. Such losses due to information asymmetry are truly regrettable.

Next, we will work with Eric to break down these 9 major "pitfalls."

First topic, the core differences between PSP and MOR, and the breakdown and evaluation of compliance costs. Eric, please start by comparing the two.

Eric

PSP stands for Payment Service Provider. In this cooperation model, the merchant itself is the primary seller of the product. MOR stands for Merchant of Record, where the payment institution acts as the core selling entity. The core differences between the two lie in responsibility attribution, risk bearing, and cost structure.

First, tax liability differs. In the PSP model, merchants must bear sales taxes in various regions globally, such as sales tax in the US, GST in Singapore, consumption tax in Japan, etc. In the MOR model, the payment institution bundles and bears these tax costs.

Second, chargeback handling responsibility differs. In the PSP model, merchants must independently handle the entire process related to chargebacks, including disputes, communication, and material collection. In the MOR model, the payment institution will initiate chargeback processing on behalf of the merchant and provide related services.

Third, fee structures differ. PSPs have a more detailed fee structure; for example, local card payments might be around 2.9%, cross-border payments around 3.4%, and currency exchange may incur additional fees. MOR, however, uses a bundled fee model, with an overall rate that is typically 0.X to 1 percentage point higher than PSPs, but it includes value-added services like tax declaration and chargeback defense.

From a cost perspective, individual developers or small and micro enterprises just starting out are more suited to the MOR model. We have encountered many overseas teams whose overseas compliance and legal fees alone amount to 10,000-20,000 RMB annually (approximately several thousand USD). If annual revenue is under 1 million RMB, this cost proportion is far higher than the additional MOR fees, making the MOR model more cost-effective in the initial stage.

However, the MOR model is not perfect. Since the payment institution acts as a "master merchant" consolidating multiple sub-merchants, if its risk control system is inadequate, any risk issue with one sub-merchant could lead to the freezing of payment accounts for all sub-merchants. In the PSP model, issues with a single merchant only affect its own account and do not involve other merchants. Therefore, MOR places higher demands on the payment institution's risk control and compliance qualifications.

Regarding compliance costs for the first overseas market, two points need attention:

First, company registration costs are similar in both models. Taking a US entity as an example, initial registration costs around a few hundred USD, with annual maintenance fees also a few hundred USD. However, beware of "low-cost registration" traps – some agencies initially charge only tens or 100 USD for registration, but annual maintenance fees skyrocket to hundreds of USD. It's advisable to clarify all front-end and back-end maintenance costs from the outset.

Second, corporate income tax and reporting requirements. Smaller developers operating in the US often choose the LLC (Limited Liability Company) model, where net profits flow through to individual income tax and are reported together, requiring two specific forms: Form 5472 and Form 1120. Enterprises with financing or capital plans will choose a corporate type company, which requires paying federal tax and corresponding state taxes.

It is crucial to emphasize that tax reporting must be timely. We have encountered cases where developers received a $25,000 penalty for not filing or paying taxes on time. While appeals are possible, the process is cumbersome and affects operations, so it must be taken seriously.

Consumption tax rates in different regions also need attention: US sales tax rates range from 4%-10% (varies by state), European VAT rates are 10%-20%+ (unified EU system), UK VAT is approximately 20%, and Japan's consumption tax is 8%-10%. Online virtual products and electronic products are all subject to the corresponding tax rates. In the PSP model, merchants must independently complete tax deduction and declaration at the point of sale; in the MOR model, the payment institution handles it centrally.

Monica

Could you further explain, based on product form and company development stage, which types of companies are more suitable for PSP and which for MOR? For example, SaaS virtual products, API products, subscription-based products, etc. Are there differences in choices at different stages?

Eric

From a product form perspective, AI, SaaS, and other products with subscription as their core billing model show no significant difference in adaptability to either model. The core criterion is not the company's size or product category, but rather whether the merchant has its own tax and overseas legal teams.

If a merchant already has a professional team to handle local tax and compliance issues, they can choose the PSP model. If they lack such a team, even with a large transaction volume, they can choose the MOR model, where the payment institution provides a safety net. Conversely, some early-stage teams whose founders have legal or tax backgrounds and can handle related matters independently can also directly choose the PSP model.

Monica

In summary, the essential difference between PSP and MOR is primarily the allocation of responsibilities and control.

Eric

That's a very precise summary.

Chargeback Rate Red Lines and Responses

Monica

At what chargeback rate will warnings be issued, or even funds frozen? How should such situations be handled? Let's start by discussing the nature of chargeback rates.

Eric

Chargeback Rate is a core indicator used by card networks and payment institutions to assess merchant risk. The logic is that if a user pays for and uses a product, then initiates a chargeback with the card network citing reasons like "unknown charges" or "unused product," and the card network or wallet approves the user's claim, it implies the merchant has risks such as over-promotion or discrepancies between product and service, leading to restrictive measures against the merchant.

While the chargeback rate red lines for major card networks like Visa and Mastercard vary, some being 0.X% and others 1%+, the core principle is "not to exceed the corresponding threshold." Once exceeded, payment institutions will first issue a warning. If the excess continues, they may impose restrictions on the account funds. Currently, virtual products and e-commerce sectors both face such risks.

Monica

When the chargeback rate reaches 1%, what is the payment institution most concerned about? Is it brand regulatory pressure or card network requirements?

Eric

From the payment institution's perspective, the core concern is the merchant's fund security and sustained operational capability. If a merchant's chargeback rate exceeds the threshold, it could lead to their account being frozen, preventing normal收款 (fund collection). As payment institutions and merchants are in a symbiotic relationship, we always hope that merchants can operate stably in the long term.

Monica

For those unfamiliar with the payment industry, let me clarify: "Card Network" refers to well-known bank card organizations like Visa and Mastercard. Let's continue to explore: if the chargeback rate exceeds the threshold, from which data dimensions will payment institutions pinpoint the problem?

Eric

First, major card networks all offer chargeback alert services. When a user initiates a chargeback (in a "dispute initiate" state), the issuing bank will first spend a few days reviewing the legitimacy of the claim – for instance, if a user has already used a service and then claims "it's not good" as a reason for chargeback, it's usually deemed unreasonable; if the user claims "I never used the product," the bank is more inclined to support the user.

Before the issuing bank formally accepts the dispute, merchants can directly issue a refund through the alert function provided by the card network. This refund will not be counted in the chargeback rate statistics. Although the cost of a refund is similar to a chargeback processing fee, it can effectively reduce the chargeback rate numerator. Some merchants can reduce their chargeback rate by 30%-80% using this feature.

For example, Visa's RDR product and Mastercard's Aftercard product are official chargeback optimization tools launched by the card networks, offering more targeted solutions.

Additionally, chargeback defense is an important method, but two points need attention: first, a defense means a chargeback record has already been generated (even if the defense is successful, it will still count towards the chargeback rate); second, it's advisable to handle disputes based on amount – directly refund small chargebacks, and for large amounts (e.g., annual subscriptions), collect sufficient evidence for defense, both protecting profits and preventing the chargeback rate from climbing excessively.

Eric

To reduce chargeback rates at the source, we first need to clarify the four core reasons for high chargeback rates: first, users perceive unexpected or unauthorized charges; second, the subscription cancellation process is cumbersome, and they can't find the entry point; third, billing information is unclear, and users cannot identify consumption items through credit card statements; fourth, customer service response efficiency is low, and user requests cannot be resolved in a timely manner.

For this, it is recommended to refer to the subscription page designs of major companies like Microsoft and Netflix, whose compliance and user experience have been market-tested, which can significantly reduce chargeback risks. If there is demand, we can discuss the specific details of optimizing the entire subscription process later.

Monica

Are there cases where the payment success rate is not low, but the account is still frozen?

Eric

This situation does exist. Payment success rate and account freezing are two independent logics: an excessive chargeback rate will lead to warnings or freezing, but account freezing can also be triggered by other reasons such as compliance issues or content risks, and payment institutions may also terminate cooperation for these reasons.

Audience

Is overseas chargeback common?

Eric

Chargeback frequency varies significantly by region. North America has a moderate chargeback rate, but users have strong payment capabilities, and overall transaction volume performs well; regions like Japan have lower chargeback rates and high user retention; some regions like South Korea have relatively higher chargeback rates.

Overall, chargebacks are an unavoidable phenomenon in cross-border payments. Mature operational teams can usually control chargeback rates to a few permille. This is related to overseas credit card consumption habits, where their popularity and history of use are longer than in China, making chargeback operations more routine for users.

Audience

How can we prevent competitors from maliciously designing chargebacks? Are there compliant methods to handle this?

Eric

Currently, among the clients we serve, there haven't been significant cases of malicious chargebacks from competitors. The core reason is that overseas payments and refunds incur fixed costs – users pay a certain handling fee when making a payment (e.g., paying $10 might incur a $0.3-$0.5 fee), and only the principal is refunded, not the handling fee. Malicious chargebacks also incur costs for the initiator, so such cases are relatively rare. If there are specific scenarios, we can analyze them further individually.

Audience

What payment methods can overseas independent websites use?

Eric

Waffo primarily focuses on online digital business payment services, and offline independent website collection is not our core expertise. From industry experience, merchants building on Shopify can use its built-in payment solutions, and Stripe also supports e-commerce clients' collection needs, for your reference.

Credit Card Dispute Handling for Virtual Products

Monica

Virtual products lack shipping tracking numbers, making the probability of losing credit card disputes almost 100%. How should this be handled? What evidence do card networks typically favor in such disputes?

Eric

Handling chargebacks for virtual products requires strategies tailored to the reason for the chargeback. The core principle is "abandon defense for small amounts, provide full evidence for large amounts." If the amount is small, spending a lot of time compiling materials is not worth it; it's advisable to directly refund through the chargeback alert function. For large orders, the following three types of core evidence can be collected for defense:

First, the user's product usage records. This is key evidence to refute "unused product" type chargebacks, such as daily login records, feature operation logs, etc.;

Second, user authorization credentials, such as fingerprint authorization upon product opening, password verification records, etc.;

Third, notification and communication records. If a user initiates a chargeback claiming "did not receive renewal reminders," email logs or other renewal notification tracking can be provided.

Distinguishing between users who "truly haven't used" and those who "have used but falsely claim not to have used" primarily relies on the aforementioned usage records and authorization credentials. This data directly reflects whether the user has actually used the product.

Monica

So, even though virtual products don't have physical logistics tracking, they still have traceable and auditable delivery and usage records, and this data needs to be retained completely in advance.

Eric

Yes, this aligns with the core logic of chargeback handling: first, determine if it's worth defending, then collect evidence specifically.

When an account is suddenly frozen, if KYB (Know Your Business) materials are required, how should risk decisions be made?

Eric

If funds have been refunded to consumers after an account freeze, they usually cannot be recovered, so prevention is far more important than remediation. Most account freezes stem from website content security issues, which are often overlooked by growing teams. The following risk points need to be carefully avoided:

First, IP infringement risk. If a website's homepage or content pages use well-known IPs like Disney or Marvel, it's highly likely to trigger lawsuits. From large enterprises to small and micro businesses, there have been cases of being sued by such companies. Once IP infringement is triggered, it often leads to high fines.

Second, adult content risk. If a website's homepage or content pages contain NSFW (Not Safe For Work) content, nudity, or suggestive language, the monitoring systems of payment institutions and card networks will quickly identify it, leading to account restrictions. This monitoring is continuous and dynamic, so violating content cannot be hidden for long.

Third, Deepfake-related risks. In recent months, North America has been extremely strict about the use of real human images, and all human-like content carries deepfake risk; among these, celebrity images have a higher risk level, and materials related to celebrities who have publicly opposed AI deepfakes (like Taylor Swift) are considered "high-risk red lines." If a product involves text-to-image generation, virtual companionship, etc., and uses such celebrity images for promotional materials, the account will likely be frozen directly, with no possibility of recovery.

Fourth, lack of reporting and review mechanisms. Websites need to set up user reporting entry points in key locations such as content pages and product cards, and clearly state the feedback processing time (SLA) in the T&C (Terms and Conditions) and privacy policy. Especially for UGC (User-Generated Content) products, user-uploaded content may pose risks. A complete reporting and review mechanism can prove that the platform has risk control capabilities, significantly reducing the probability of account freezing.

Why is the payment success rate lower in India, Brazil, and Southeast Asia?

Eric

Differences in payment success rates across regions primarily stem from three factors: economic level, payment penetration, and payment method adaptability.

From the perspective of recommended regions for overseas expansion, North America is the first choice due to strong user spending power and mature consumption habits; East Asian regions (e.g., Japan) have decent initial purchase conversion rates and extremely high user retention, making the ROI (Return on Investment) considerable from an LTV (Lifetime Value) perspective; Europe and Latin America (e.g., Brazil) are growing faster, with local spending power and usage habits gradually maturing, making them suitable for second-tier deployment.

Specifically regarding payment success rate differences: first, some regions have relatively lower economic levels, limiting user spending power; second, card payment penetration is much lower than in North America, with many users using wallets or debit cards, which often leads to payment failures due to insufficient balance; third, payment method adaptability significantly impacts success rates. Pure card payments generally have a success rate of about 75%-85%, while local wallet payments are often between 50%-70%, a clear difference.

Monica

For these markets, how should we balance risk control intensity and traffic conversion? Should we choose to increase risk control for security, or lower risk control for conversion?

Eric

From a payment perspective, we observe three mainstream strategies:

First, focus on a single high-value market. Most companies prioritize deep cultivation in the North American market, where operational costs are relatively controllable – if the volume in other regions is only 20%-30% of North America, but requires the same operational costs, the ROI (Return on Investment) will significantly decrease.

Second, dynamic decision-making based on ROI. The core is to calculate the full-link costs and benefits: front-end traffic acquisition costs, first-purchase costs, renewal rates, etc. It's crucial to determine the user payback period (industry standard is 3-6 months, no more than 1 year). If the payback period in a certain region exceeds 5 years, it does not have investment value from a business perspective.

Third, avoid virtual credit card "freeloading" risks. In some regions, the credit system is imperfect, allowing users to easily apply for virtual credit cards. They can register multiple accounts to repeatedly use free quotas, causing merchants to bear high costs without actual revenue. For such regions, it's crucial to specifically avoid the abuse of virtual credit cards, without over-focusing on the balance between risk control and conversion.

Dealing with Virtual Card "Freeloading"

Monica

Virtual cards are non-physical credit card numbers generated by banks, which can be used temporarily, have spending limits, or be canceled at any time. This lowers the barrier for user trials but also amplifies the uncertainty of trial-to-paid conversion. How can we control such risks through mechanism design and qualification verification?

Eric

A protection system can be built from three dimensions:

First, set up a small pre-authorization. Charging a very small pre-authorization fee, such as $1, can filter out a large number of single-use virtual cards without funds. If users are unwilling to pay a small pre-authorization fee, either the product is not attractive enough, or they are not target users. The loss of such users has limited impact on the business.

Second, blacklist interception. Payment institutions usually accumulate a list of virtual card numbers. Merchants can apply to add these card numbers to a blacklist, directly refusing payments. Although this might lose a small number of genuine users, it significantly improves ROI in scenarios where virtual card abuse is high.

Third, optimize the trial mechanism. Limit the frequency of trials to prevent the same user from repeatedly trying; add verification steps like CAPTCHA to increase the barrier for account registration. The core is to identify virtual card abuse risks early by observing data through gradual rollout, rather than fully opening trials from the initial launch.

The Nature, Significance, and Core Role of 3DS (3D Secure Verification), and What Problem It Solves

Eric

The core value of 3DS is to verify that the payment action is performed by the user themselves, thereby reducing the risk of fraud and chargeback liability. If 3DS is not enabled, once issues like fraud occur, banks often side with the user's claim, and the merchant bears the chargeback loss; at the same time, an increased chargeback rate leads card networks and payment institutions to deem the merchant high-risk, forming a vicious cycle.

With 3DS enabled, users need to complete payment through additional steps like SMS verification, which effectively confirms that the payment is made by the cardholder. Even if the user later initiates a chargeback claiming "unauthorized payment," its legitimacy will be significantly reduced, substantially protecting merchant interests.

Currently, 3DS supports flexible configuration: for trusted users, the verification process can be simplified to ensure smooth payments; for high-risk scenarios (e.g., using a VPN, IP address inconsistent with card's country of origin, like a US IP using a Russian card for payment), verification can be strengthened to balance business and risk control. The core goal is to minimize the impact on conversion while controlling chargeback rates and abuse risks.

Monica

After enabling 3DS, conversion will see a certain decline. From your observation, which type of users are mainly lost?

Eric

Firstly, abusive accounts (such as fraud, malicious trials) will be completely blocked; secondly, some users will abandon payment due to cumbersome verification processes (e.g., phone not nearby to receive verification code) or poor operational experience. From an internet product logic perspective, any additional step will lead to conversion loss, which is an unavoidable objective phenomenon.

Monica

I heard that 3DS 2.0 has a frictionless authentication feature. Eric, do you know the details?

Eric

I haven't deeply researched these details yet. We will arrange for our team to compile the core features of 3DS 2.0 and share them in the communication group later. Interested individuals can join the community to get the information.

Cracking the "Black Box" of Payment Success Rate

Monica

How can we transform an uncontrollable success rate into an observable and iteratively optimizable metric?

Eric

Payment success rate is not uncontrollable; the core is to improve it through detailed breakdown and targeted optimization. Most payment service providers offer success rate reports, but standardized reports often lack sufficient granularity, typically showing only an overall success rate (e.g., 75%) without explaining the specific reasons for the 25% failed orders, making it difficult for merchants to optimize.

In our practice, we primarily improve success rates through three methods:

First, a retry mechanism for insufficient balance scenarios. For regions or user groups with a high proportion of insufficient balances, regularly initiating payment retries, especially around user paydays when funds are abundant, can recover 2%-3% of failed orders.

Second, specifically addressing bank integration issues. There was a case where a merchant experienced an extremely high rejection rate for transactions with a certain medium-sized bank in North America. After investigation, it was found that the billing description field was not transmitted correctly, causing the bank to default to rejecting the transaction. By optimizing the API field transmission, the rejection rate for that bank significantly decreased, successfully recovering about 1% of the success rate.

Third, multi-channel adaptation for large transactions. For scenarios such as card errors or exceeding limits, switching payment channels or sending exclusive payment links (adapted for large transactions) can improve the payment success rate in special circumstances.

Standardized products can solve 75%-80% of basic problems. To increase from 80% to 85%, specific scenarios need to be tackled one by one. Especially for domestic developers or teams with major company backgrounds, refined operations are a core advantage – improving the success rate by a few tenths of a percentage point each year can create a significant competitive barrier. After all, with fixed front-end traffic costs, a 5% increase in success rate directly means a 5% increase in ROI.

Monica

To improve the success rate, what are some actionable suggestions for event tracking in the payment flow?

Eric

Event tracking in the payment flow can be designed using a "three-layer funnel" to precisely identify problematic stages:

First layer, the conversion funnel from the payment page to the checkout page. This tracks the proportion of users who click from the pricing page (paywall) to the checkout page, primarily reflecting user payment intent – if a large number of users drop off at this stage, the pricing strategy or payment page guidance logic needs optimization.

Second layer, the conversion funnel from the checkout page to payment submission. This tracks the proportion of users who select a payment method, fill in card information, and then submit payment, primarily reflecting the convenience of payment operations – if the drop-off rate is high, the checkout page design or payment process complexity needs optimization.

Third layer, the success rate after payment submission (second-stage success rate). This tracks the proportion of failures after a payment request is submitted, due to insufficient balance, risk control interception, channel issues, etc., primarily reflecting the adaptability of the payment channel and risk control strategy – this part is the core responsibility of the payment institution and a critical stage for success rate optimization.

After breaking down the data into these three layers, issues at each stage can be targeted for optimization, leading to continuous iteration of the success rate.

Monica

It seems that payment success rate is not a fixed KPI, but rather a systematic project that requires continuous iteration.

Eric

Yes, the core is "comparing with oneself." As long as the success rate improves compared to before, whether it's 0.1 percentage point or 1 percentage point, it's an enhancement of business competitiveness.

Where do hidden fees typically appear in payment rates?

Eric

The core issue with hidden fees lies in opaque fee structures. Some payment institutions highlight basic rates (e.g., 2.9%) on their pricing pages but conceal multiple additional fees within lengthy terms and conditions. If merchants only compare basic rates, they can easily fall into the trap of "seemingly low price but actually high cost."

The most straightforward comparison method is "end-to-end accounting": calculate the actual amount received for every 100 units of a $9.9 product sold. Regardless of how complex the fee terms are, the actual amount received is the most critical criterion – if Service Provider A receives $888 for selling $999, and Service Provider B receives $880, the true cost difference between the two becomes clear at a glance.

If it's not possible to integrate multiple service providers simultaneously, focus on the following 7 types of potential hidden fees:

First, international card surcharge. The 2.9% rate advertised by some institutions may only apply to local card payments, while international cards (in cross-border payment scenarios) require an additional charge of about 0.5%, increasing the actual rate to around 3.4%.

Second, currency conversion fees. If the sales region and the settlement currency are inconsistent (e.g., selling in the US but settling in HKD), currency exchange will incur exchange rate differences, and these costs are often not reflected in the basic rate.

Third, 3DS and chargeback-related fees. 3DS verification costs approximately $0.1-$0.3 per transaction, and chargeback processing also incurs fixed fees. Some institutions do not clearly disclose these individual charges.

Fourth, excessive retry fees. Some institutions, in pursuit of "superficial success rates," repeatedly retry orders that are already confirmed to be unpayable. Each retry incurs a handling fee, leading merchants to bear meaningless costs.

Fifth, refund processing fees. When a refund is issued, only the principal is returned to the user; the handling fee from the payment stage is not refunded, and some institutions charge an additional refund processing fee. These costs need to be calculated in advance.

Sixth, chargeback penalties. Once a chargeback occurs, regardless of whether the defense is successful, some institutions charge a chargeback penalty of $15-$25. If the chargeback rate is high, these fees can significantly increase costs.

Seventh, withdrawal fees. Some service providers have low rates for the acquiring process but charge additional fees for withdrawals. It's important to consider the "acquiring + withdrawal" full-link cost.

These fees may not be "maliciously hidden," but they are often scattered across different terms. Merchants need to verify them one by one during financial reconciliation to avoid profit judgment errors due to cost calculation discrepancies.

The cost of payment errors varies greatly for businesses at different stages. At the enterprise level, there have been cases where parameter configuration errors led to out-of-control subsidy costs, resulting in losses of tens of millions or even hundreds of millions of dollars. Such losses are usually borne by the payment company itself.

From merchant cases, most costs stem from compliance issues. Many merchants have been fined tens of thousands of dollars by card networks due to a lack of website content compliance, while their own annual revenue might be less than a million dollars. Such fines can be a fatal blow to small and micro enterprises.

While the huge losses of large enterprises are astonishing, they don't necessarily affect overall operations; however, for small and medium-sized developers or teams, neglecting payment compliance, risk control, and other details can lead to costs far exceeding expectations. This is also the core reason why we are sharing these "pitfalls" today – we hope to help everyone avoid such preventable losses in advance.

Monica

Second question, if you could only prioritize one among "success rate, chargeback rate, freezing risk, compliance cost," what would be your order?

Eric

The core priority is "survival first, then growth."

The first priority is chargeback rate control, which is the "survival baseline." Once the chargeback rate exceeds the threshold, merchants will be unable to conduct normal sales, and all subsequent operations will be impossible. Therefore, the chargeback rate must be kept within the range accepted by card networks and payment institutions.

The second priority is success rate and retention rate, which are the "core of growth." The first purchase rate and retention rate directly determine LTV. The common industry break-even point is: 60%-70% first purchase rate and 40% retention rate. Achieving this level generally covers operational costs, and anything beyond that is profit.

Simply put, first ensure "you can sell," then pursue "selling more and selling longer."

Monica

Third question, from the perspective of a payment institution, what do you think are the three most important things a founder needs to do right, and the three most important things to avoid?

Eric

The three most important things to do right:

First, choose a reliable payment partner. Developers usually don't invest much time in the payment sector, so finding a payment institution that can cover compliance, risk control, and after-sales can significantly reduce the chances of falling into pitfalls, allowing the team to focus on core businesses like product and growth.

Second, pay attention to payment-related detail management. Even with a partner providing support, aspects related to funds still require personal attention – payment directly affects the company's cash flow, and any oversight in detail can lead to irreversible losses.

Third, cultivate a long-term operational mindset. Payment compliance and risk control efforts may not yield immediate results, but they ensure stable long-term business operations, preventing short-term gains from overshadowing long-term risks.

The three most important things to avoid:

First, crossing legal red lines. Child pornography is an absolute red line with zero tolerance globally; once involved, all payment tools will be immediately removed, and severe legal liabilities will be incurred.

Second, neglecting content compliance risks. IP infringement, illegal adult content, high-risk deepfake materials, etc., may seem like "small issues" but can easily lead to account freezing or hefty fines. A content review mechanism needs to be established in advance.

Third, choosing gray or illicit channels/products. While such collaborations might seem to offer quick profits, fund security is completely unprotected, and long-term operations are impossible, ultimately leading to greater costs.

PSP 与 MOR 的核心区别

Monica

今天为大家邀请到的嘉宾是 Waffo 的 CMO Eric 褚泳成。

Monica

感谢 Eric 的分享。今天我们不聚焦 “选择哪个 PSP 更便宜” 这类表层问题，而是深入跨境支付的 “黑洞区域”：为何同款产品，有人支付成功率能突破 90%，有人却卡在 60%-70%？很多团队会先归咎于产品或流量，但实则问题出在支付端。这种因信息不对称导致的损失，其实非常可惜。

接下来，我们就与 Eric 一同拆解这 9 大 “坑”。

首先第一个话题，PSP 与 MOR 的核心区别，以及合规成本的拆解与评估，有请 Eric 从两者对比展开分享。

Eric

PSP 即 Payment Service Provider（支付服务商），合作模式下，商户自身是产品销售主体；MOR 即 Merchant of Record（大商户模式），支付机构将作为核心销售主体，两者的核心差异集中在责任归属、风险承担及成本结构上。

第一，税务责任不同。PSP 模式下，商户需自行承担全球各地的销售税，例如美国的 sales tax、新加坡的 GST、日本的消费税等；MOR 模式下，支付机构会打包承担这些税务成本。

第二，拒付处理责任不同。PSP 模式中，商户需自行处理拒付相关的抗辩、争议沟通及材料收集等全流程；MOR 模式下，支付机构将代为发起拒付处理并提供相关服务。

第三，费率结构不同。PSP 的费率体系较为细致，例如本地卡支付费率约 2.9%，跨境支付约 3.4%，且换汇等环节可能产生额外费用；MOR 则采用打包费率模式，整体费率比 PSP 高零点几到 1 个百分点，但已包含税务申报、拒付抗辩等增值服务。

从成本角度看，刚起步的个人开发者或小微企业更适合 MOR 模式。我们接触过不少出海团队，仅海外合规及律师费用每年就需 1-2 万元人民币（约几千美金），若年营收在 100 万元人民币以内，这部分成本占比远高于 MOR 的额外费率，因此起步阶段 MOR 模式性价比更高。

但 MOR 模式并非完美。由于支付机构作为 “大商户” 整合多个子商户，若其风控体系不足，任一子商户出现风险问题，都可能导致所有子商户的支付账户被冻结。而 PSP 模式下，单个商户出现问题仅影响自身账户，不会牵连其他商户，因此 MOR 对支付机构的风控及合规资质要求更高。

关于出海首站的合规成本，需注意两点：

其一，公司注册成本在两种模式下差异不大，以美国主体为例，首次注册费用约几百美金，次年维护费用同样为小几百美金，但需警惕 “低价注册” 陷阱 —— 部分机构初期仅收几十或 100 美金注册费，次年维护费却暴涨至数百美金，建议初期就明确前后端所有维护成本。

其二，企业所得税与申报要求。体量较小的开发者在美国展业时，多选择 LLC（有限责任公司）模式，净利润将穿透至个人所得税合并申报，需填写 form 5472 和 form 1120 两张专属表格；有融资或资本规划的企业，会选择 corporate 类型公司，需缴纳联邦税及对应州税。

需重点提醒的是，税务申报必须及时。我们曾遇到开发者因未及时申报或缴纳税收，收到 25000 美金罚单的案例，虽可申诉，但流程繁琐且影响运营，务必重视。

不同地区的消费税税率也需关注：美国 sales tax 税率为 4%-10%（各州不同），欧洲 VAT 税率为 10%-20%+（欧盟统一体系），英国 VAT 约 20%，日本消费税为 8%-10%，线上虚拟产品及电子产品均需按对应税率缴纳。PSP 模式下，商户需自行完成销售环节的税费扣除及税务申报；MOR 模式下，支付机构将统一处理。

Monica

能否结合产品形态及企业发展阶段，进一步说明哪些类型的企业更适合 PSP，哪些更适合 MOR？比如 SaaS 虚拟产品、API 类产品、会员制产品等，不同阶段的选择是否有差异？

Eric

从产品形态看，AI、SaaS 等以订阅制为核心收费模式的产品，对两种模式的适配性无明显差异。核心判断标准并非企业体量或产品品类，而是商户是否具备自有税务及海外法务团队。

若商户已有专业团队可处理本地税务、合规等问题，可选择 PSP 模式；若缺乏相关团队，即使交易额较大，也可选择 MOR 模式由支付机构兜底；反之，部分初期团队若创始人具备法务或税务背景，能自主处理相关事务，也可直接选择 PSP 模式。

Monica

综上，PSP 与 MOR 的本质差异，核心是责任归属与控制权的划分。

Eric

这个总结非常精准。

支付拒付率的红线与应对

Monica

拒付率超过多少会收到警告，甚至面临资金冻结？遇到这类情况该如何处理？我们可以先从拒付率的本质聊起。

Eric

拒付率（Chargeback Rate）是卡组（Card Network）及支付机构评估商户风险的核心指标。其逻辑是，用户支付并使用产品后，若以 “未知费用”“未使用产品” 等为由向卡组发起拒付，卡组或钱包若认可用户诉求，会判定商户存在过度宣传或产品与服务不符的风险，进而对商户采取限制措施。

Visa、Mastercard 等主流卡组的拒付率红线虽有差异，部分为 0. 几 %，部分为 1%+，但核心原则是 “不能超过对应阈值”。一旦超标，支付机构会先发出警告，若持续超标，可能对账户资金采取限制措施，目前虚拟产品及电商领域均存在此类风险。

Monica

当拒付率达到 1% 时，支付机构最担心的是什么？是品牌监管压力还是卡组的要求？

Eric

站在支付机构角度，核心担心的是商户资金安全及持续运营能力。若商户拒付率超标，可能导致其账户被冻结，无法正常收款，而支付机构与商户属于合作共生关系，我们始终希望商户能长期稳定运营。

Monica

这里为不熟悉支付领域的同学补充说明：卡组即 Card Network，也就是大家熟知的 Visa、Mastercard 等银行卡组织。接下来我们继续探讨，若出现拒付率超标，支付机构会从哪些数据维度定位问题？

Eric

首先，主流卡组均提供拒付预警服务。当用户发起拒付（处于 dispute initiate 状态），发卡行会先花几天时间审核诉求合理性 —— 例如用户已使用服务后以 “不好用” 为由拒付，通常会被判定为不合理；若用户声称 “未使用过产品”，则银行更倾向于支持用户。

在发卡行正式受理前，商户可通过卡组提供的预警功能直接发起退款，这笔退款不会计入拒付率统计。虽退款成本与拒付处理费用相近，但能有效降低拒付率分子，部分商户通过该功能可将拒付率降低 30%-80%。

例如 Visa 的 RDR 产品、Mastercard 的 aftercard 产品，均是卡组官方推出的拒付优化工具，针对性更强。

此外，拒付抗辩也是重要手段，但需注意两点：一是抗辩会导致拒付记录已产生（即使抗辩成功，仍会计入拒付率）；二是建议按金额分级处理 —— 小额拒付直接退款，大额（如年付订单）可收集充分证据进行抗辩，既保障利润，又避免拒付率过度攀升。

Eric

想要从源头降低拒付率，需先明确高拒付率的四大核心原因：一是用户认为存在意外或未授权收费；二是订阅取消流程繁琐，找不到入口；三是账单信息不透明，用户无法通过信用卡账单识别消费项目；四是客户服务响应效率低，用户诉求无法及时解决。

对此，建议参考 Microsoft、Netflix 等大厂的订阅页面设计，其合规性及用户体验均经过市场验证，可大幅降低拒付风险。具体的订阅全流程优化细节，若大家有需求可后续深入交流。

Monica

有没有支付成功率不高，但账户仍被冻结的案例？

Eric

这种情况确实存在。支付成功率与账户冻结是两个独立逻辑：拒付率超标会导致警告或冻结，但账户冻结还可能因合规问题、内容风险等其他原因触发，支付机构同样可能因此解除合作。

观众

海外拒付现象常见吗？

Eric

拒付频率存在明显地域差异。北美地区拒付率处于中等水平，但用户付费能力强，整体交易额表现亮眼；日本等地区拒付率较低，且用户留存率高；韩国等部分地区拒付率则相对较高。

从整体来看，拒付是跨境支付中无法完全避免的现象，运营成熟的团队通常能将拒付率控制在千分之几的水平。这与海外信用卡消费习惯相关，其普及程度及使用历史均长于国内，拒付操作对用户而言更为常规。

观众

如何防止竞争对手恶意设计拒付，是否有合规操作方法？

Eric

目前我们服务的客户中，尚未出现明显的竞争对手恶意拒付案例。核心原因在于，海外支付及退款存在固定成本 —— 用户支付时需承担一定手续费（例如支付 10 美金需支付 0.3-0.5 美金手续费），退款时仅退还本金，手续费不予返还。恶意拒付对发起方而言同样存在成本，因此这类案例相对少见。若有具体场景，可单独拎出来进一步分析。

观众

境外独立站可以使用哪些收款方式？

Eric

Waffo 主要聚焦线上数字业务支付服务，线下独立站收款领域并非我们的核心专长。从行业经验来看，Shopify 建站的商户可使用其自带支付解决方案，Stripe 也支持电商类客户的收款需求，供大家参考。

虚拟产品的信用卡争议处理

Monica

虚拟产品无物流单号，面临信用卡争议时，败诉概率几乎接近 100%，该如何应对？卡组在这类争议中更认可哪些证据？

Eric

虚拟产品的拒付应对需结合拒付原因制定策略，核心原则是 “小额放弃抗辩，大额充分举证”。若金额较小，花费大量时间整理材料得不偿失，建议通过拒付预警功能直接退款；若为大额订单，可收集以下三类核心证据进行抗辩：

第一，用户的产品使用记录，这是反驳 “未使用产品” 类拒付的关键证据，例如每日登录记录、功能操作日志等；

第二，用户授权凭证，例如产品打开时的指纹授权、密码验证记录等；

第三，通知与沟通记录，若用户以 “未收到续费提醒” 为由拒付，可提供邮件等续费通知的追踪日志。

区分用户 “真未使用” 与 “已使用但谎称未使用”，核心依据就是上述使用记录与授权凭证，这些数据能直接反映用户是否实际使用产品。

Monica

也就是说，虚拟产品虽无物流轨迹，但仍有可追踪、可审计的交付与使用痕迹，这类数据需提前留存完整。

Eric

是的，这与拒付应对的核心逻辑一脉相承：先判断是否值得抗辩，再针对性收集证据。

账户突然被冻结时，若需补充 KYB（企业尽调）材料，该如何进行风险决策？

Eric

账户冻结后若资金已退还给消费者，通常无法追回，因此预防远比补救更重要。多数账户冻结源于网站内容安全问题，这类问题往往被增长中的团队忽视，以下几类风险点需重点规避：

第一，IP 侵权风险。网站首页或内容页若使用迪士尼、漫威等知名 IP ，极易引发起诉。从大企业到小微企业，都有过被这类企业起诉的案例，IP 侵权一旦触发，往往面临高额罚款。

第二，成人内容风险。若网站首页或内容页存在 NSFW（不适合工作场合）内容、裸露画面或相关引导性词语，支付机构及卡组的监测系统会快速识别，进而限制账户。这类监测是持续动态的，违规内容无法长期隐藏。

第三，Deep fake 相关风险。近几个月北美地区对真人形象的使用管控极为严格，所有真人类型内容都存在deep fake 风险；其中，名人形象的风险等级更高，而公开表态过反对 AI deep fake 的明星（如 Taylor Swift）相关素材，属于 “高危红线”。若产品涉及文生图、虚拟陪伴等场景，使用这类明星形象作为宣传素材，账户大概率会被直接冻结，且无挽回余地。

第四，举报与审核机制缺失。网站需在内容页、产品卡片等关键位置设置用户举报入口，并在 TNC（服务条款）及隐私政策中明确反馈处理时限（SLA）。尤其对于 UGC 模式的产品，用户上传的内容可能存在风险，完善的举报与审核机制能证明平台具备风险管控能力，可大幅降低账户冻结概率。

为何印度、巴西及东南亚地区的付款成功率较低？

Eric

不同地区的支付成功率差异，核心源于三个因素：经济水平、支付渗透率及支付方式适配性。

从出海地区选择建议来看，北美是首选，用户付费能力强、消费习惯成熟；东亚地区（如日本）首购转化率尚可，且用户留存率极高，从 LTV（用户终身价值）角度看，投入产出比可观；欧洲及拉美地区（如巴西）增速较快，本地消费能力与使用习惯逐步成熟，可作为第二梯队布局。

具体到支付成功率差异：其一，部分地区经济水平相对较低，用户付费能力有限；其二，卡支付渗透率远低于北美，大量用户使用钱包或借记卡支付，易出现余额不足等支付失败场景；其三，支付方式适配性影响显著，纯卡支付的整体成功率约 75%-85%，而本地钱包支付成功率多在 50%-70% 之间，差异明显。

Monica

针对这些市场，该如何平衡风控强度与流量转化？是选择提高风控换安全性，还是降低风控换转化？

Eric

从支付角度，我们观察到三种主流策略：

第一，聚焦单一高价值市场。多数公司会优先深耕北美市场，其运营成本相对可控 —— 若其他地区的体量仅为北美市场的 20%-30%，但需投入同等运营成本，ROI（投资回报率）会显著降低。

第二，基于 ROI 动态决策。核心是计算全链路成本与收益：前端投流成本、首次购买成本、续订率等数据，需明确用户回本周期（行业常见 3-6 个月，最长不超过 1 年）。若某地区回本周期超过 5 年，从商业角度看并不具备投入价值。

第三，规避虚拟信用卡薅羊毛风险。部分地区信用体系不完善，用户可轻松申请虚拟信用卡（Virtual credit card），通过注册多个账号反复使用免费额度，导致商户承担高额成本却无实际收益。针对这类地区，需重点规避虚拟信用卡滥用风险，无需过度纠结风控与转化的平衡。

虚拟卡 “白嫖” 的应对

Monica

虚拟卡是银行生成的非实体信用卡卡号，可临时使用、限额控制或随时作废，降低了用户试用门槛，但也放大了试用转付费的不确定性。该如何通过机制设计与资格校验控制这类风险？

Eric

可从三个维度构建防护体系：

第一，设置小额预授权。收取 1 美金等极低额度的预授权费用，可过滤大量未充值的一次性虚拟卡。若用户不愿支付小额预授权，要么是产品吸引力不足，要么并非目标用户，这类用户的流失对业务影响有限。

第二，黑名单拦截。支付机构通常会积累虚拟卡卡号清单，商户可申请将这类卡号加入黑名单，直接拒绝支付。虽可能损失少量真实用户，但在虚拟卡滥用率较高的场景下，对 ROI 的提升效果显著。

第三，优化试用机制。限制试用频次，避免同一用户重复试用；增加验证码等校验环节，提升账号注册门槛。核心是通过逐步放量观察数据，提前识别虚拟卡滥用风险，而非上线初期就全面开放试用。

3DS（3D 安全验证）的本质、意义及核心作用，它到底解决了什么问题？

Eric

3DS 的核心价值是核实支付行为为用户本人操作，进而降低盗刷风险与拒付责任。若未开启 3DS，一旦出现盗刷等问题，银行多会采信用户诉求，商户需承担拒付损失；同时，拒付率的提升会让卡组及支付机构判定商户风险较高，形成恶性循环。

开启 3DS 后，用户需通过短信验证等额外步骤完成支付，能有效确认支付为本人操作。即便后续用户发起 “非本人支付” 的拒付诉求，其合理性也会大幅降低，可显著保护商户权益。

目前 3DS 支持灵活配置：对信任用户可简化验证流程，保障支付顺畅；对高风险场景（如使用 VPN、IP 与卡号归属地不一致，例：美国 IP 使用俄罗斯卡支付）可强化验证，实现业务与风控的平衡。核心目标是在控制拒付率与滥用风险的前提下，尽可能降低对转化的影响。

Monica

开启 3DS 后，转化会有一定下滑，从你的观察来看，主要流失的是哪类用户？

Eric

首先，滥用账号（如盗刷、恶意试用）会被完全拦截；其次，部分用户会因验证流程繁琐（如手机不在身边无法接收验证码）或操作体验不佳而放弃支付。从互联网产品逻辑来看，任何额外步骤都会产生转化折损，这是无法避免的客观现象。

Monica

听说 3DS 2.0 具备无感验证功能，Eric 是否了解相关细节？

Eric

这部分细节我暂未深入研究，我们会安排团队整理 3DS 2.0 的核心特性，后续在交流群中分享，感兴趣的同学可加入社群获取。

支付成功率的 “黑盒” 破解

Monica

如何将不可控的成功率，转化为可观测、可迭代的优化项？

Eric

支付成功率并非不可控，核心是通过精细化拆解与针对性优化实现提升。多数支付服务商都会提供成功率报告，但标准化报告的颗粒度不足，往往只显示整体成功率（如 75%），却无法说明 25% 失败订单的具体原因，导致商户难以优化。

我们的实践中，主要通过三类方式提升成功率：

第一，针对余额不足场景的重试机制。对余额不足比例较高的地区或用户群体，定期发起支付重试，抓住用户发薪日等资金充裕的节点，可追回 2%-3% 的失败订单。

第二，针对性解决银行对接问题。曾有商户在北美某中型银行的交易拒绝率极高，排查后发现是账单描述字段未正确传输，导致银行默认拒绝交易。通过优化 API 字段传输，该银行的拒绝率大幅下降，成功追回 1% 左右的成功率。

第三，多渠道适配大额交易。针对卡片异常、超出限额等场景，切换支付渠道或发送专属支付链接（适配大额交易），提升特殊场景的支付成功率。

标准化产品能解决 75%-80% 的基础问题，若要从 80% 提升至 85%，则需要针对具体场景逐一突破。尤其对于国内开发者或大厂背景的团队，精细化运营是核心优势 —— 每年将成功率提升零点几个百分点，就能形成显著的竞争壁垒。毕竟，前端流量成本固定的情况下，成功率提升 5%，就意味着 ROI 直接提升 5%。

Monica

为提升成功率，在支付链路上做事件埋点，有哪些可落地的建议？

Eric

支付链路的埋点可按 “三层漏斗” 设计，精准定位问题环节：

第一层，付费页到收银台的转化漏斗。统计用户从定价页（付费墙）点击进入收银台的比例，核心反映用户支付意愿 —— 若大量用户在此环节流失，需优化定价策略或付费页引导逻辑。

第二层，收银台到支付提交的转化漏斗。统计用户选择支付方式、填写卡信息后提交支付的比例，核心反映支付操作的便捷性 —— 若流失率高，需优化收银台界面设计或支付流程复杂度。

第三层，支付提交后的成功率（二段成功率）。统计提交支付请求后，因余额不足、风控拦截、渠道问题等导致的失败比例，核心反映支付渠道与风控策略的适配性 —— 这部分是支付机构的核心责任，也是成功率优化的关键环节。

三层漏斗的数据拆分后，可针对性优化各环节问题，实现成功率的持续迭代。

Monica

可见支付成功率并非固定 KPI，而是需要持续迭代的系统工程。

Eric

是的，核心是 “与自己对比”。只要成功率较之前有优化，无论是 0.1 个百分点还是 1 个百分点，都是业务竞争力的提升。

支付费率中的隐藏收费，通常会出现在哪些环节？

Eric

隐藏收费的核心问题在于费率结构不透明，部分支付机构会在定价页突出基础费率（如 2.9%），却将多项额外费用隐藏在冗长的条款中。商户若仅对比基础费率，很容易陷入 “看似低价实则高价” 的陷阱。

最直观的对比方式是 “端到端核算”：统计每卖出 100 份 9.9 美金的产品，最终实际到账金额。无论费率条款如何复杂，实际到账金额是最核心的判断标准 —— 若 A 服务商卖出 999 美金到账 888 美金，B 服务商到账 880 美金，两者的真实成本差异一目了然。

若无法同时接入多家服务商，可重点关注以下 7 类潜在隐藏费用：

第一，国际卡附加费。部分机构宣传的 2.9% 费率仅针对本地卡支付，国际卡（跨境支付场景）需额外加收 0.5% 左右的费用，导致实际费率提升至 3.4% 左右。

第二，货币转换费。若销售地区与收款币种不一致（如美国地区销售，收款为港币），换汇环节会产生汇差成本，这部分费用往往未在基础费率中体现。

第三，3DS 及拒付相关费用。3DS 验证每笔约收取 0.1-0.3 美金，拒付处理也会产生固定费用，部分机构未明确告知这类单项收费。

第四，过度重试费用。部分机构为追求 “表面成功率”，会对已确定无法支付的订单反复重试，每笔重试都会产生手续费，导致商户承担无意义的成本。

第五，退款手续费。退款时仅返还用户本金，支付环节的手续费不予退还，且部分机构会额外收取退款处理费，这部分成本需提前核算。

第六，拒付罚款。一旦发生拒付，无论抗辩成功与否，部分机构会收取 15-25 美金的拒付罚款，若拒付率较高，这部分费用会显著增加成本。

第七，提现费用。部分服务商在收单环节费率较低，但提现时会收取额外费用，需关注 “收单 + 提现” 的全链路成本。

这些费用未必是 “恶意隐藏”，但往往分散在不同条款中，商户需在财务对账时逐一核实，避免因成本核算偏差影响盈利判断。

三个灵魂追问

Monica

感谢 Eric 的详细拆解。最后，我们补充三个关键问题。

第一个问题，结合你十多年的跨境支付从业经验，见过的最昂贵的支付错误是什么？造成了哪些代价？

Eric

不同阶段的企业，支付错误的代价差异极大。大厂层面，曾出现因参数配置错误导致补贴成本失控的案例，损失金额达上千万甚至上亿美金，这类损失通常由支付公司自行承担。

从商户案例来看，更多代价源于合规问题。不少商户因网站内容合规缺失，被卡组罚款数万美金，而其自身年营收可能不足百万美金，这类罚款对小微企业而言，往往是致命打击。

大厂的巨额损失虽惊人，但不至于影响整体经营；而中小开发者或团队，若忽视支付合规、风控等细节问题，可能付出远超预期的代价。这也是我们今天分享这些 “坑” 的核心原因 —— 希望帮助大家提前规避这类可避免的损失。

Monica

第二个问题，若只能在 “成功率、拒付率、冻结风险、合规成本” 中优先保障一个，你的排序是什么？

Eric

核心排序是 “先保生存，再求增长”。

第一优先级是拒付率控制，这是 “生存底线”。拒付率一旦超标，商户将无法正常开展销售，后续所有运营都无从谈起，因此必须将拒付率控制在卡组及支付机构认可的范围内。

第二优先级是成功率与留存率，这是 “增长核心”。首购率与留存率直接决定 LTV，行业常见的盈亏平衡线是：首购率 60%-70%，留存率 40%，达到这一水平，基本能覆盖运营成本，超出部分即为利润。

简单来说，先确保 “能卖”，再追求 “卖得多、卖得久”。

Monica

第三个问题，站在支付机构角度，你认为创始人最需要做对的三件事，以及最需要规避的三件事是什么？

Eric

最需要做对的三件事：

第一，选择靠谱的支付合作伙伴。开发者通常不会投入过多时间在支付领域，因此找到能兜底合规、风控、售后的支付机构，能大幅减少踩坑概率，让团队聚焦产品、增长等核心业务。

第二，重视支付相关的细节管理。即便有合作伙伴兜底，与资金相关的环节仍需亲自关注 —— 支付直接影响公司现金流，任何细节疏忽都可能导致不可逆的损失。

第三，建立长期主义的经营心态。支付合规、风险控制等工作无法立竿见影，但能保障业务长期稳定运营，避免因短期利益忽视长期风险。

最需要规避的三件事：

第一，触碰法律红线。儿童色情是全球零容忍的绝对红线，一旦涉及，所有支付工具都会立即下架，且需承担严重法律责任。

第二，忽视内容合规风险。IP 侵权、违规成人内容、高危 deep fake 素材等，看似是 “小问题”，但极易引发账户冻结或高额罚款，需提前建立内容审核机制。

第三，选择黑灰产通道或产品。这类合作看似能快速获利，但资金安全毫无保障，且无法实现长期经营，最终会付出更大代价。
