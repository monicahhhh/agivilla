Monica Wang Founder, AGI Villa
BAI Capital: Since 2008, the BAI Capital team has invested in over 200 companies, achieving 18 IPOs and more than 40 unicorns. Their investment focus includes the internationalization of Chinese industries, leveraging advantages in technology, talent, and supply chains to drive breakthroughs in retail, fintech, content innovation, Web3, and more. BAI Capital's predecessor was Bertelsmann Asia Investments, part of the Bertelsmann Group — an international media, services, and education giant with nearly 200 years of history, operations in 50 countries, and approximately 80,000 employees.

Thirteen Years of Investing — What Stages Have You Gone Through?

Will Wang:

Our fund started in 2008, and my investment career began in 2012. The first phase was mobile internet — we're application investors by origin. Back then, the apps included KEEP, Tantan, and other mobile internet applications — dating, communities — they became subscription or e-commerce business models. That was basically the first wave of mobile internet.

Later, there was a wave of industrial internet companies — B2B commerce. Then came the so-called sharing economy, like Mobike. We had Hellobike, which we invested in during that era.

After that, everyone started chasing the last tail of e-commerce — for example, Dingdong Maicai and the fresh grocery wave. After that ended, around 2020, everyone started investing in consumer brands. One of our best-performing companies is BOP, which does oral care — mouth sprays, toothpaste. It's probably number one on Douyin now, with excellent growth and operations.

Then for a while we were involved in blockchain-related things. Web3 was hot for a while, then it crashed. We entered the industry after Web3 had its problems — we thought it was a very cyclical industry. But through Web3's rise, when we looked at what was actually worth studying behind it, we saw blockchain acceleration, global de-SWIFTification, and many people wanting to bypass VISA — that's the stablecoin trend everyone's watching now. We actually started investing in some players and companies two or three years ago. We even set up a fund overseas to invest in European and American projects, because a lot of deregulation in this space started in the West.

Then starting two years ago, we looked at AI — specifically this wave of Transformer-based AI. We didn't invest in the 'Six Little Dragons.' Our investments are basically all application companies, many of which we just started investing in over the past six months. We've probably issued six or seven term sheets in the past two months, with many projects in the process of closing — all application-focused.

Beyond my personal track record, our fund has two other major threads. One is what we call 'the Chinese American Dream' — since 2008. For example, we invested in Yiche and NIO founder Li Bin. Projects he was involved with — Uxin, Mobike — were all highly related to Li Bin. He was essentially co-founder or founder of these projects.

We also looked at things from the perspective of daily life — food, clothing, transportation. In between, we invested in some autonomous driving and enterprise SaaS, but I think our deepest insights come from fintech.

We look at business models globally and found a major trend: financial inclusion — letting people who previously couldn't access banking services get access. In this trend, we invested in fintech unicorns in Mexico, Asia, and Africa. So fintech is one of our main threads. The stablecoin work I mentioned is also an extension of fintech. So overall, BAI Capital's investments cover three areas: fintech, consumer, and AI.

What Triggered Your Focus on AI?

Monica:

What first drew your attention to AI?

Will Wang:

The first AI company I encountered in my career was probably Face++, back when I first joined the company in 2012-2013. Then autonomous driving was a wave. Before this Transformer wave, there were GAN-based generation companies — we invested in companies that generated fashion designs for others.

We really started engaging with Transformer AI around GPT-3 and GPT-4. Before GPT-3, many founders were already telling us that game script writing could use AI. Then GPT-3.5 plus ChatGPT was when everyone saw it.

So we really started diving deep during GPT-3.5, but we didn't start getting a real feel for applications until after DeepSeek.

AI Investment in Four Categories: Media Disruption, Industry Transformation, China Advantage, Super Human

Monica:

What kind of projects have you invested in over the past two months?

Will Wang:

I categorize our AI investments into four types.

The first is Media Disruption — primarily looking at text, audio, video, and 3D gaming from a media medium perspective. We've found that in China's video AI space, among the global top 5-6 apps, about three are made by Chinese or Chinese-origin teams. This is very different from the AI coding landscape.

The main reason is that many models have already reached SOTA, but user needs aren't SOTA — they're layered. In these areas where user needs don't chase SOTA but we've already reached it, we can bring down the entire cost structure and rapidly provide easily accessible services.

So many video generation tools aren't trying to produce Hollywood blockbusters — they generate short, fun content. Chinese-made products are extremely strong in this area.

In the video space, we invested in what we call a 'Cursor for Video.' We believe that among Adobe's 30+ million paying users worldwide — similar in number to the world's programmers — those users also need something like Cursor to save time. So we invested in what we call, in quotes, 'Cursor for Video.'

In image and 3D communities, we already have portfolio companies including TensorArt and LibLib — image community platforms doing very well with massive overseas traffic. Then there's Meshy AI, founded by Hu Yuanming (original Taichi Engine founder), a text-to-3D platform for game developers. So from images to video to 3D assets, we've already invested across the board.

Why do I mention this first? Because it's closely related to Bertelsmann's business. Among Bertelsmann's $20 billion global portfolio, the largest segment is TV stations, streaming, and MCN. Second is publishing — Penguin Random House, the world's largest publisher. Third is BMG, the music rights company. The entire books-film-music industry will be impacted by AI — some parts disrupted, some enhanced.

The second theme is AI Rollup — the AI upgrade, transformation, and acceleration of traditional companies or existing internet businesses. Here we pursue network effects. If AI can strengthen businesses that already have network effects or two-sided platforms, that's very powerful. Most VC returns over the past 10-20 years have come from network effects.

The third theme is China Advantage. Two examples: consumer electronics — we invested in VITURE (AR glasses) and Looki (AI-enhanced outdoor cameras). We're watching categories that ship tens of millions of units annually — headphones, glasses, watches — and their AI transformation. Another category is the next-generation SHEIN based on China's flexible supply chain, where the backend speed and efficiency are enhanced but the frontend personalization is taken to the extreme.

The last direction — and what we'll discuss today — is Super Human. We believe solo unicorns will emerge and solo companies will be empowered. But more importantly, it's not about creating unicorns — it's that work methods will fundamentally change.

In this changing work paradigm, there will be many 'pick-and-shovel' businesses. If I truly want to run a company in a highly efficient, lean way — what do I need? Do I need a labor market? Hiring HR, PR, GR, admin? Can I get some of these basic white-collar functions from agents rather than people? So we invested in a company that empowers this trend.

To summarize in English: Media Disruption, AI Rollup, China Advantage, and Super Human. But if I had to pick just one word instead of four directions, I'd say AI is achieving ultimate personalization — similar to how collaborative filtering algorithms personalized media through recommendation engines like Douyin. We still believe that in media, communities, two-sided platforms, and even basic information transmission, there will be radical personalization transformations.

The Investment Logic Behind Solo Companies

Monica:

What stage are most of these AI companies you've invested in, and what's their scale?

Will Wang:

We have angel-round companies valued at around $10 million, some even smaller. Then growth-stage companies valued at $200-300 million. Our individual investment amounts range from as small as $1.5 million to as large as $20-30 million.

Monica:

What about the Super Human / solo company space?

Will Wang:

For solo companies and solo unicorn investments, we're still designing the amount and proportion, because there's a fundamental question: invest too little and it's meaningless — you might as well give them operational resources. Invest too much and the money doesn't really help them.

We think there will be a new investment instrument — similar to how General Catalyst invested in Grammarly's last round. It might be investing in your growth. You need money for growth, and I'll support you. This could be something like RBF (Revenue-Based Financing) — investing in the calculable ROI portion of your revenue growth, then sharing in the returns rather than taking equity.

Pre-PMF products in the solo unicorn stage should just use cloud providers for support — covering basic development costs. I think as long as someone can afford McDonald's every day to feed themselves, they should be able to use Cursor to write code and build things.

So before the product is ready, I think there should be a period of public resource support — maybe incubators or accelerators. Once the product is ready for promotion and scaling, that's when you need financial products and services. But is it still VC? I think the VC investment model — the amounts, closing process, complexity — even simplified, isn't really suitable for solo companies.

Will Solo Companies Kill VC?

Monica:

The investment model for solo companies still seems like a question mark.

Will Wang:

Right. My vision is that solo companies are a good thing — they maximize an individual's leverage and returns. You're just one person, you don't split with anyone, so from a distribution perspective it's maximized. If your capabilities are also amplified, your returns are maximized too.

So VC has no role here. If an individual makes an AI product — like Cal AI, the calorie counting app — and earns several million dollars, who do they split with? They don't need to. They don't need investment. That's great for them.

But you might say capitalism doesn't seem to play a role here. I think it's like having a Douyin account that does well — you can earn millions too. If we assume that Xiaohongshu follower count equals monthly income — 10,000 followers means 10,000 RMB per month, 1 million followers means about 10 million RMB per year, roughly $1.5 million. Why would you split with a VC?

This fundamentally changes production relations because productive forces have been massively unleashed. You don't need to be employed by others, and you don't need to employ others. You participate in social collaboration, using platform resources on open platforms to achieve personal financial growth. That's a great thing.

Monica:

Will solo companies kill VC?

Will Wang:

No, because human-to-human collaboration can't be eliminated just because AI amplifies individual capabilities. Today's interview has to happen between you and me, right? If a machine conducted this interview, the content probably wouldn't be something listeners would want to hear.

Human-to-human collaboration will remain very important — even more important. When your individual impact is amplified through AI, it's still a linear relationship. The non-linear growth that capital seeks must come from collaboration.

A solo company's capabilities mainly come from their creativity and their extraordinary mental fortitude and execution. But they have a ceiling — their personal time — and a clear risk — they're alone.

So from a VC perspective, we want to invest in teams. If these highly capable solo founders can team up — even just two people creates a squared relationship, three people creates a cubed relationship. The exponential growth from three people multiplied together is the source of returns we're seeking — that's non-linear growth.

Monica:

What's the most perfect combination of three people?

Will Wang:

A team like Manus — three people from different backgrounds, each with their own strong suits, all with strong execution and hands-on ability. Together they can build a product that, in an era where everyone's talking about the model bulldozer pushing from behind, can actually run faster than the model. I think that's very typical.

Monica:

What's the biggest threshold or challenge for solo companies? Product level, market level, technical level, or the human level?

Will Wang:

It's actually very interesting. For product-focused solo companies, you're working for users, working for yourself — not for a boss. So the biggest challenge is this state change: I have to work for myself. People who work for themselves need strong mental fortitude.

You need powerful mental strength to constantly battle with Cursor — it's not a perfect tool. You have to keep iterating, overturning your previous work, setting your own work plans, releasing products with rhythm, setting your own deadlines, and using different tools. You don't know things, so you have to learn — and your learning time in this state far exceeds what it was at a big company.

That's why we want to build the Vibecorn community. People need to come out and share. People are lonely. The hardest part is that nobody's cracking the whip behind you — isn't it easy to give up? I think the psychological aspect of working alone is really important.

Founder Spectrum: Ambitious Visionaries, Young Geniuses, Star Students, Ruthless Operators

Will Wang:

Our classification goes like this: Ambitious Visionaries, Young Geniuses, Star Students, and Ruthless Operators. But being an Ambitious Visionary is the most essential, non-negotiable condition.

The other three multiply with it. You can be a Young Genius times an Ambitious Visionary — a Young Genius is someone who achieves beyond their age. A Ruthless Operator is someone who'll stop at nothing to achieve their goal — that's a neutral description. A Star Student is someone from a big company — a P-level from ByteDance, Stanford, etc.

Star Students are easy to invest in when everyone's lost. Young Geniuses are who you invest in when everyone's feeling adventurous. Ruthless Operators are hard for most funds to invest in because they easily challenge your moral bottom line or values on the surface.

But I believe the most important thing is being an Ambitious Visionary. If you're not ambitious, being a Young Genius alone isn't enough.

What is ambition? There are different manifestations. Some ambition looks like: 'I've already achieved financial freedom, but I want to go higher, do something bigger, so I'm temporarily giving up my comfortable lifestyle.' Another form: 'To accomplish something great, I've brought my social relationships, my credit, my best friends, my most respected people into my company as co-founders, asking them to sacrifice their work and lifestyle to start a business with me.' And there's a pure, passionate form: 'As an individual facing a social problem or business opportunity, I feel duty-bound — if I don't do this, I'll be angry, because this thing needs to happen and I didn't make it happen.' A founder with that kind of righteous anger.

Three Fundamental Beliefs: Intelligence, Collaboration, Open Source

Monica:

You've previously mentioned three beliefs in AI investing: believing in large models, believing in young people, and believing in open source. But in reality, large model development has hit bottlenecks, young entrepreneurs' success rates remain very low, and open source faces commercialization challenges. Are these three beliefs too idealistic?

Will Wang:

After that podcast, we already revised one — we changed 'believe in young people' because we got criticized heavily, mostly by older people, haha.

Based on first principles, our current fundamental laws are: believe in intelligence, believe in collaboration, and believe in open source.

Believing in intelligence essentially means: when you want to scale or grow your business, your first recourse should be the power of intelligence and productivity, not hiring another person. We think many applications are built ahead of intelligence, and when intelligence reaches a certain level, it lifts those applications up. Cursor wasn't built just now — it was built three years ago, before the models. When Anthropic's coding capabilities got strong, it lifted Cursor up.

We changed 'believe in young people' because we've since seen many cases of experienced people whose experience combined with AI produces incredible results. If they're young at heart, still learning fast, and mentally strong, their capabilities absolutely exceed those of young people.

Believing in collaboration means: how do you acquire resources? Some say through internal company growth. We believe the solo company ideology means acquiring resources through industry-wide collaboration. If our community enables 1,000 solo companies to gather, surely some of them can collaborate as upstream and downstream partners?

We emphasize collaboration between solo companies — we think this will be very powerful. We don't think a solo company should always be just one person. Collaboration between them — one plus one is definitely greater than two, maybe even greater than ten.

Believing in open source: people always think of open source as giving back or contributing, but it's not. Open source means remembering that accumulation and success are often built on the shoulders of those who came before.

Take the blockchain industry as an example. Most VC-invested companies have no technical contribution — they're essentially marketing companies. But why does the industry keep advancing technically? Ethereum, Layer 2, rollups — much of this isn't done by people in companies. It's done by open-source developers who work during the day and tinker with crypto at night. The entire blockchain industry is essentially the monetization of an open-source ecosystem.

The Ethereum Foundation is a group of only 200-300 people maintaining a $300-400 billion economic system. Including applications built on top, it's probably a $500-600 billion economy. Each person is maintaining roughly $1-2 billion worth of economic activity.

In AI, I believe there will also emerge a massive infrastructure and network contributed and maintained by open-source contributors. Without open source, if everyone has to rely on others' services in purely commercial relationships, I don't believe individuals or super intelligence can develop with peace of mind.

Based on these three fundamental laws — believe in intelligence, believe in collaboration, believe in open source — like the Taoist saying 'three begets all things,' many things will emerge from these principles.

AI 投资四大类：媒体颠覆、产业改造、中国优势、一人公司

Monica：

最近两个月出手的项目是什么样的？

Will 汪天凡：

我把我们投的 AI 的项目分为四类啊。

第一类呢，就是叫做 MEDIA DISRUPTION，这个媒体的颠覆。其实主要是跟文字，音频，视频，三维游戏这些从媒体介质角度去看。所以我们发现目前来讲中国的视频 AI 的领域在全球能够可能 TOP 五，TOP 六的 APP 里面可能有三个是中文或者华人做的。这个其实跟我们在 AI CODING 里面的局面很不一样啊。

我觉得主要的原因就是有很多的模型我们已经追到 SOTA 了，但是用户的需求不是 SOTA 的，用户的需求是分层的，所以在这种领域，我们可以用户需求没有那么追求 SOTA。但是我们已经追到 SOTA 的情况下，我们把整个这个成本结构 COST DOWN 下来，迅速提供用户可以很容易获得的服务。

所以你去看很多视频生成，没有说要生成好莱坞大片，都生成短的，好玩儿的啊。这块其实国人做的产品是非常强的。

在视频领域，目前我们投了一个，我们把它叫做有点像 CURSOR FOR VIDEO。我们认为在 VIDEO 里面，全世界 ADOBE 的付费用户也有三千多万，程序员也有三千多万。我们认为 ADOBE 的三千多万的支付费用户也需要一个类似于像 CURSOR 帮你省时间，所以我们投了一个带引号的，叫做 CURSOR FOR VIDEO。就是在多模态里面，然后呢，在图片社区或者在三维社区里面，其实我们都已经有了 PORTFOLIO 包括 TENSOR ART，LIBLIB。大家都比较知晓的，就是图片社区这一类，我们有家公司做得不错啊，在海外的量非常大。然后另外就是 Meshy AI，是 HU YUANMING 做的，原来太极引擎的 FOUNDER HU YUANMING 做的一个文生 3D，或者说给游戏开发者和公司做的这个三维素材生成的一个平台，所以从图片到这个视频到三维素材我们其实都已经有所投资。

为什么我先讲这个，是因为它跟我们贝塔斯曼的业务是很相关，因为贝塔斯曼在全球最主要的两百亿美金的构成里面最大的一块是电视台、流媒体 MCN。第二名是这个出版业务，企鹅兰登书屋，全世界最大出版社，第三是 BMG 版权音乐公司。所以书影音整套行业一定会被 AI 影响，你不能说颠覆，也许有些部分是颠覆，有些是影响，有些是加强。

第二个点呢，我们主打的一个主题叫做 AI ROLLUP，这大家提的很多，就是传统公司或者现有的互联网业务的 AI 升级改造和加速，这里面我们追求的是网络效应，就是说，如果 AI 能够使本身就有网络效应或者双边平台的业务更加强的话，那个是很厉害的。因为大部分 VC 过去十几二十年所获得的回报收益大头都来自于网络效应。所以我们会投一些类似于跟比如随便说 KOL MATCHING 相关的一些平台，对吧，双边网络，就是这个对接人和商家，对接人和卖家或者怎么样的这样的一些些网络啊。我举个例子，还有一些可能跟线下的这个销售网络搭建有关的一些跟 AI 相关的事情。所以这个是叫做 AI ROLLUP。

第三个主题呢，叫做 CHINA ADVANTAGE，就是中国优势。我举两个例子，一是消费电子，那这个在里面我们投了 VITURE，做 AR 眼镜的，它可能之后也很快会有一个 AI 的产品。另外呢，我们投了 Looki，一个做类似于户外相机的 AI 化，那这样的两家公司，我们也是差不多，应该是在最近，有一家是我们增持了很多。另一家也是最近又交割了新的一轮。这两个方向是跟消费电子的相关。那消费电子的其实大家看到很多，耳机，眼镜，手表，就是很多人都在看，所以我们也在看那些每年能够出货上亿上千万的品类的这个所谓的 AI 化的变化。还有一类基于中国供应链，比如说非常灵活的中国供应链所能诞生的，更新时代的 SHEIN，这个项目还不好说。那它更多的有点像就是说，如果 SHEIN 基于中国的供应链，能够把这个后端的速度效率给提升，但前端的个性化还没有做到极致，所以我们会投一家在全球电商里面可能把前端的个性化也做到极致的一个公司。所以这个叫做 CHINA ADVANTAGE。

最后一个我们投的方向，今天我们也会讨论，叫做 SUPER HUMAN。我们认为一人独角兽会诞生，一人公司会被加强。但更重要的不是说要诞生独角兽或者什么样的公司，而是更重要的是工作方式会被改变。在这种工作方式改变下，会有很多卖水的人的生意，就是说，如果我真的想要以非常高效和 LEAN 的方式来做一家公司的话。我需要什么样的东西？我是不是需要一个劳动力市场？招我的 HR，招我的 PR，招我的 GR，招我的什么样的同事、员工？ADMIN、有一些基础的白领岗位，我可不可以从 AGENT 获取，而不是从人那儿获取？所以我们投了一家类似于来 EMPOWER 这样的一个趋势的公司。

所以总结来讲就是，用英文说，就是叫是 MEDIA DISRUPTION、媒体颠覆，然后 AI ROLLUP、AI 对业务的改造，CHINA ADVANTAGE、中国的优势发挥，最后是 SUPER HUMAN、就是一人独角兽的诞生，这个是我们过去这两个月所看到的项目可被归的类。但我觉得呢，我比较喜欢拎出来的一个词儿是，我觉得 AI 确实正在做的一件事情是极致个性化。如果真的只要拎一个词儿，而不是四个方向，我们认为 AI 的极致个性化这件事情就和当年协同过滤算法对于这个媒体的这个个性化，什么大家看抖音啊、这些推荐算法引擎等等是一样的影响。我们仍然认为，在媒体，社区，双边平台，甚至最基础的信息传递领域，都会出现极致个性化的改造。

还有一种野心的体现是这种很纯真的热爱，是说我作为一个个体，面对社会问题或者商业机会的时候，我发现责无旁贷、非我其谁，就是一定这个事情得我做，如果我不做，我会很生气，因为如果这件事情这么需要发生、但我没让它发生，而自带一种愤怒感的这种 FOUNDER。我至少看到过这三种人，我觉得这是我对于野心的粗浅理解。

三大信仰：相信智能、相信协作、相信开源

Monica：

你在之前的播客里有提到 AI 投资的三大信仰：相信大模型，相信年轻人，相信开源。但是现实中呢，其实大模型的发展遇到了瓶颈，年轻人的创业成功的概率依然是很低的，开源也面临着商业化的困境。所以这三个信仰是不是太理想化了呢？

Will 汪天凡：

首先我们那个播客录制之后就已经修正了一条，把相信年轻人改掉了，被骂死了，哈哈。大多是年纪大的人来骂我。我们后来自己基于第一性原理去想事儿。首先啊，我们现在的基本法是相信智能，相信协作和相信开源。

来解释一下，相信模型、相信智能还是差不多的例子啊，我们的理解叫做，相信智能本质上是说，当你想要 SCALE 或者增长你的业务的时候，你首先诉诸的是智能的力量、生产力的来源，而不是诉诸于多雇一个人啊，这个是相信智能的意思，或者多做一些工程上的事儿、去弥补智能的不足。我们觉得很多应用是做在智能之前的，然后当智能的达到一定水平的时候，会把你这个应用给带起来。所以大家看到的 CURSOR 现在到这个水平，它也不是此刻才做的，三年前就已经做了，所以他是在模型之前做的。anthropic 模型能力强，CODING 能力强了之后，会把它一下子带起来啊。

从大的角度来讲，如果你都不相信智能，你投什么 AI，或者你做什么 AI 呢？那你相信啥呢？你相信人，那相信人，那你就去回到之前的阿里巴巴时代的中共铁军、商业管理学啊。那你相信人的话，那你的组织。移动互联网时代结束，就 PC 互联网、移动互联网结束，大的公司的人口就是十几二十万嘛。那你相信人？你是不是要把自己做成富士康？你要变成一百万一千万？你相信人嘛，对吧？所以在组织层面其实相信智能，其实是在组织层面的一个第一的一条基本法。

把相信年轻人改掉的原因，就是因为我们认为他甚至跟年轻、年长无关。因为我们后来又见到非常多的案例，是很有经验的人，他的经验和 AI 之间结合，看到一些苗头。所以我们认为确实就是，如果虽然年长，但是心态年轻，学习能力依旧强，心力十足，我觉得他们的发挥的能力绝对绝对比年轻人还要厉害。所以我们认为心态年轻很重要，但不是说要相信年轻人本身，所以会有很大的误解。

后来我们觉得很重要的一个点，被我们遗漏的是相信协作。什么叫相信协作？我刚才第一条是讲组织，对吧？第二条就是说你怎么样去获得资源。有的人是说我获得资源来自于我公司内生，我们认为一人公司或者这种一人公司的意识形态是说，我获得资源的方式是跟全行业合作。那如果这个世界上因为我们的社区而实现一千个一人公司的聚会的话，这一千个里面总有你的上下游可以合作吧？我们强调的是一人公司之间的协作，我们认为这个会很强大，而且甚至里面暗含了一条暗线，我们希望这种协作能够让他们组队，就我们并不觉得一人公司本身就该永远是一个人，就这个一这个数字那么重要，不是的。我们认为协作，他们之间的协作一加一一定大于都不止二，可能一加一都大于十了，所以这个是协作。

第三个，相信开源，相信开源的意义，它是一种，大家总把开源想成一种回馈或者贡献方式，其实不是，开源就是说一定要记住，很多时候事情的积累和成功是基于前人种树、后人乘凉的。

我们拿区块链行业举个例子，区块链这个行业，大部分的 VC 投资公司是没有技术贡献，他们本质上都是营销公司，但是为什么这个行业从技术角度来讲还在不断地进步？大家在谈的什么以太坊，以太坊二层啊，ROLLUP 啊什么的一大堆事情，本质上很多时候不是在公司里的人做的。而是都是开源的开发者贡献都是大家白天在上班，晚上回去倒腾加密的这些事情贡献的。整个区块链行业，其实就是一个开源生态的变现，所以这个开源的生态非常了不起，没有人需要重复再做一条以太坊，但是你去以太坊上做出来的各种应用。在这些应用上面，他们还有很多是协议合约，你在上面再做应用，就这里面出来的那些力量，在过去的五年，我看的觉得非常的令人振奋。

我觉得这里面导致的一个很有意思的结果就是以太坊基金会是一个只有两三百人的团体，他们在维系的是一个三千亿到四千亿美金体量的经济体。算上上面其他的应用，可能是五六千亿的经济体。所以你可以理解为每个人在维护接近一到两个 BILLION，就是十到二十亿美金体量的经济体。按照以太坊的 STAKING 的 REVENUE 来算的话，它十到二十亿美金一年都能贡献至少两三千万美金的这个计算的费用的收入，就相当于人均两三千万美金收入。

我觉得在 AI 里面，一定还是会出现一个由开源贡献者贡献的，维护的一个巨大的基础设施和网络的。所以我觉得大家要对这个有信心，因为有了这个之后，协作和个体智能才能够发生啊。因为如果没有开源的话，所有人要基于他人的服务，大家的关系是一种商业利益的互相咬合的关系的话，我并不认为个体或者超级智能能够安心的发展。开源为什么如此重要，是在这儿。

所以这个是我们认为，基于这三条基本法，我们说相信智能、相信协作、相信开源这三条基本法来工作的话。可以像道家一样的三生万物，就在这个基本法上，很多东西都会诞生。所以我们在做的那个社区就一个理念，我们不是一个 VC 要做一个东西，只是为了我们去宣传 VC 自己，不需要，我们想把相信这三条基本法的人都聚在一起。我们认为这三个东西乘起来会出来很多特别有意思的事情。这三件事情不是我们发明的，这个事情在区块链加密行业，在过去中本聪 08 年到现在，已经发生过了。

Monica：

你刚才提到相信协作，能不能举一个能够产生于一加一大于十的类型？

Will 汪天凡：

我举个例子，就是如果有一家一人公司，它在上游、它做的是电商，它卖货的，那是个卖货的公司，它需要找 KOL。另外一家一人公司是专门做 KOL 的一个一人 AGENT，这两个人就是可以协作的，你本质上其实相当于这个公司的这个产品销售组织和这个公司的营销推广啊，这个公司的产品供应链团队和一个公司的营销推广团队之间的合作。

Monica：

感谢 Will 带来的这场思想风暴。当我们抽离细节，会发现这场对话始终在围绕「人」的层面。从「野心家」的必然性到「心力」的稀缺性，从一人独角兽的杠杆效应到组队的指数级突破——技术释放了生产力，但真正定义未来的，仍是人类对自我极限的挑战。
