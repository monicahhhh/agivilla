Monica Wang Founder, AGI Villa

On March 10, 2026, Eridu, a startup operating in stealth until then, announced it had emerged from stealth and closed a $200 million oversubscribed Series A round. Its investor lineup is star-studded: Socratic Partners led the round, with legendary investor John Doerr participating personally, and quant giants Hudson River Trading, Capricorn Investment Group, and Matter Venture Partners joining as co-investors. Strategic backers include TSMC (via its VentureTech Alliance arm), MediaTek, Bosch Ventures, and TDK Ventures. Total funding raised now stands at $230 million — and the product has not yet officially launched.

What does it do? In one sentence: building network switches for AI data centers. More precisely: redesigning an entirely new network architecture from the chip up, to boost communication speeds between GPUs by an order of magnitude, while cutting power consumption by 70% and costs by 40%.

Stories of startups raising $200 million before product launch were not uncommon in 2026. But Eridu’s founder is no young Stanford dropout. Drew Perkins, 62, began his career in 1984. He co-authored PPP (Point-to-Point Protocol), a foundational internet protocol that forms a critical part of the TCP/IP suite. He has founded or co-founded five companies, four of which were acquired or went public, with aggregate exit valuations exceeding $3 billion.

Unlike vibe-driven products built by 20-something dropouts, Eridu’s founder possesses something increasingly rare in Silicon Valley today: deep, decades-long expertise.

What is a 62-year-old networking veteran setting out to achieve at the height of the AI boom? And why are investors pouring $200 million into a company with no public product?

Product Logic: GPUs Are Waiting on Networks — Networks Are the Bottleneck

First, the problem: what’s known as the Network Wall.

In 2024, the world spent hundreds of billions of dollars on AI data centers. GPUs kept getting more powerful — from 4 PFLOPS in 2022 to over 20 PFLOPS today, with performance increasing tenfold each year. But raw GPU power means little without fast communication between them. Training a large foundation model does not rely on a single GPU; it requires the coordination of thousands, even tens of thousands of GPUs. They must perform gradient all-reduce, model-layer synchronization, and KV-cache exchange — all operations dependent on networking.

The issue is this: GPU performance improves 10x annually, while network switches only see 2–3x gains every 2–3 years. It’s like owning a Ferrari but being forced to drive it on a country dirt road. GPUs are starved for network bandwidth. Networking has become the bottleneck.

Drew Perkins put it bluntly: “Billions of dollars in AI data center investments are being wasted because networks can’t keep up. The improvement rates promised by incumbent vendors are nowhere near sufficient.”

Today’s data center networks rely on Leaf-Spine Architecture — stacked layers of switches. Need more bandwidth? Add more switches. But each additional layer adds another hop for data, increasing latency. Worse, every layer requires a large number of optical transceivers, the most unreliable and power-hungry component in the entire network.

The result? The scale-up domain (a tightly coupled GPU cluster) in modern AI training systems is capped at roughly 72 GPUs. Beyond that size, network latency spikes and GPU utilization plummets. In a cluster of 200,000 GPUs, a 10% increase in GPU utilization translates to over $1 billion in savings.

Eridu’s solution is not incremental tinkering on existing architectures. It is reimagining the entire network from the chip level.

Its core product is a High-Radix Switch. Conventional switches have a limited number of ports (e.g., 64 ports), requiring layers of switches to connect large GPU fleets. Eridu’s switch increases port count by an order of magnitude — one Eridu switch replaces 30 traditional switches.

What does this mean? A GPU cluster that once required three layers of switches now needs just one. Data travels from one GPU to another in a single hop, rather than three to five. Latency drops drastically, power use falls by 70%, and hardware costs shrink by 40%.

More crucially, the scale: Eridu claims its architecture supports scale-up domains of thousands of GPUs (up from 72) and scale-out domains of over one million GPUs. In the AI data center arms race, a gap of this magnitude means the difference between being able to train frontier models — and not being able to at all.

The “Final Battle” of a 62-Year-Old Serial Entrepreneur

To understand why investors bet $200 million pre-launch, you must understand Drew Perkins.

Born in 1963, he graduated from Carnegie Mellon University (CMU) in 1986 with three degrees: in electrical engineering, computer engineering, and mathematics. During his time at CMU, he accomplished two landmark feats: first, building the CMU PC/IP software stack, which enabled ordinary DOS computers to connect to the internet; second, co-authoring the PPP protocol (RFC 1134/1171). For context: before broadband, nearly everyone who used dial-up internet relied on PPP. It is part of the internet’s foundational infrastructure.

His entrepreneurial career followed:

  • 1998: Co-founded Lightera Networks, a optical switching firm, acquired by Ciena for over $500 million in 1999.

  • 2000: Co-founded OnFiber Communications, a fiber-to-the-business provider, acquired by Qwest for $107 million in 2006.

  • 2001: Co-founded Infinera, a pioneer in large-scale photonic integrated circuits, which went public in 2007 and was acquired by Nokia for $2.3 billion in 2025.

  • 2013: Co-founded Gainspeed, a cable photonics networking company, later acquired by Nokia.

  • 2015: Co-founded Mojo Vision, an AR contact lens startup.

Five companies, four successful exits. Total exit value exceeds $3 billion. A perfect serial entrepreneurship record of this kind is extremely rare in Silicon Valley.

A turning point came in February 2023. Perkins spoke with Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI, at a small private gathering. Altman told him: “What made AI and ChatGPT possible was massive compute power.” At the time, Altman referenced 4,000 GPUs. Today, we speak in millions of GPUs.

Perkins immediately realized the bottleneck would not be the chips themselves, but how chips communicate with one another. With 40 years of experience building networking infrastructure — from PPP to optical switching to photonic chips — he understood the limits of networking better than anyone.

In late 2023, he met Omar Hassen. Hassen previously served as a switch architect at Broadcom (working on the first high-density 10G Ethernet switch), led product management at Marvell, served as VP/GM at AppliedMicro, and co-founded Entridia, a semiconductor company. He brings over 25 years of experience in networking chip design.

In 2024, the two, alongside a third co-founder Mike Capuano (leading marketing), established Eridu in Saratoga, Silicon Valley.

The fundraising process speaks volumes. Perkins made a few calls to old friends — including Wen Hsieh, Founding Partner at Matter Venture Partners and former head of KPCB China. Hsieh invested, then passed the opportunity to John Doerr, the legendary KPCB investor who had backed Perkins’ prior companies. Doerr signed on immediately. What followed was a scramble for allocation. “My phone wouldn’t stop ringing,” Perkins recalled. “The round was heavily oversubscribed.”

Competitive Landscape

The AI data center networking market is currently dominated by a handful of giants. Broadcom rules the Ethernet switching chip market, with its Tomahawk 5 and Jericho3-AI families serving as industry standards, used by Arista, Cisco, and others. Nvidia builds both GPUs and networking gear: its InfiniBand (Quantum series) has long ruled high-performance computing networks, and its Spectrum-X line competes in Ethernet. Cisco recently launched its own AI networking chip, Silicon One G300. Marvell is also a strong competitor.

A major industry shift is underway: Ethernet is recapturing market share from InfiniBand. By 2025, Ethernet accounted for more than two-thirds of AI data center back-end networks, up from less than half previously. The Ultra Ethernet Consortium (UEC) released its 1.0 specification in June 2025. The entire industry is moving away from Nvidia’s closed ecosystem toward an open Ethernet model.

Eridu occupies a unique position: it is not choosing sides between Ethernet and InfiniBand, but redesigning switches from the silicon level. It partners with TSMC to manufacture chips using cutting-edge process and packaging technologies, rather than iterating on 20-year-old architectures like Broadcom. The company currently employs around 100 people, with plans to grow to 170 by year-end, 130 of whom are based at its Saratoga headquarters.

Market size: The AI data center networking market is projected to exceed $50 billion in 2026 and could reach $200 billion by 2032. It is a market where the more you spend on GPUs, the more you must spend on networking.

Key Takeaways

First, the value of experience. In recent years, Silicon Valley has fixated on young founders — 20-something dropouts coding in dorm rooms and reaching unicorn status in three years. It’s a glamorous narrative, but Drew Perkins embodies an alternative entrepreneurial logic: someone with 40 years of infrastructure expertise who, when confronted with a new need, can rethink systems from first principles. He isn’t learning how to build switches — he co-wrote internet protocols, built photonic chips, and understands the hard limits of the physical layer. This kind of deep experience has become the scarcest resource in the AI era.

Second, the Network Wall concept. Most discussions of AI infrastructure focus on GPUs — whose chips are faster, whose compute is greater. But Eridu highlights a frequently overlooked truth: GPU performance is no longer the bottleneck. Communication between chips is. This mirrors the evolution of the smartphone industry: once processor performance plateaued, competition shifted to communication protocols (5G), thermal design, and power efficiency. AI infrastructure is undergoing the same transition — from a “compute race” to a “connectivity race.”

Third, the clean-sheet design strategy. Broadcom’s switching chip architecture has evolved over 20 years, with each improvement layered onto legacy designs. Eridu is starting from scratch: new silicon architecture, new packaging, new system design. It is a high-risk, high-reward bet: success could leapfrog 20 years of incumbent progress; failure could burn through $200 million with nothing to show.

Last, the investor structure. Eridu’s backers are not a typical VC portfolio — they resemble a supply chain alliance. TSMC (chip manufacturing), MediaTek (networking chip expert), Bosch Ventures and TDK Ventures (sensor and electronics giants) all invested. This means Eridu has been building its production supply chain since day one. This is no PowerPoint startup — its investors are its suppliers.

Closing Thought

Return to where we began: a 62-year-old networking veteran who started writing foundational internet protocols in 1984 and built five companies over 40 years. In February 2023, he had a brief conversation with Sam Altman. Altman said AI runs on massive compute. Perkins instantly understood: the bottleneck is not chips, but networks. GPUs get 10x faster every year; networks only 2–3x every 2–3 years. The gap will only widen until the entire AI industry is trapped by the Network Wall.

So he did something old-school in Silicon Valley: he didn’t build an app, nor train a model. He redesigned network switches from the chip up. One switch replaces 30. Power down 70%, cost down 40%. Before the product even launched, TSMC invested, John Doerr invested, and a $200 million round was heavily oversubscribed.

In an industry that worships 20-year-old founders, the market is placing its biggest bet yet on deep experience.

2026年3月10日，一家叫Eridu的公司宣布从隐匿模式中走出，同时完成2亿美元A轮融资（超额认购）。投资方阵容很夸张：Socratic Partners领投，传奇投资人John Doerr个人参投，量化巨头Hudson River Trading、Capricorn Investment Group、Matter Venture Partners跟投。战略投资方包括TSMC（台积电，通过旗下VentureTech Alliance参投）、MediaTek（联发科）、Bosch Ventures（博世）、TDK Ventures。累计融资2.3亿美元。产品还没有正式发布。

做什么的？一句话：给AI数据中心做网络交换机。更精确地说：从芯片开始重新设计一种全新的网络架构，让GPU之间的通信速度提升一个数量级，同时把功耗降低70%、成本降低40%。

这种“产品还没出来就拿了2亿美元”的事，在2026年并不罕见。但Eridu的创始人不是一个刚从斯坦福辍学的年轻人。Drew Perkins，62岁，职业生涯从1984年开始。他写过互联网底层协议PPP（Point-to-Point Protocol），这个协议是TCP/IP的关键组成部分。他创办或联合创办过5家公司，其中4家被收购或上市，累计退出金额超过30亿美元。

和一个20多岁辍学生搞的vibe-coded产品不同，Eridu的创始人拥有硅谷如今越来越稀缺的东西：深度经验。

一个62岁的网络老兵，在AI最火的时候，要去做什么？为什么投资人愿意在产品还没出来的时候就给2亿美元？

产品逻辑：GPU在等网络，网络在拖后腿

先说问题——这个问题叫“网络墙”（Network Wall）。

2024年，全球在AI数据中心上砸了几千亿美元。GPU越来越强——从2022年的4 PFLOPS到现在的20+ PFLOPS，性能每年翻10倍。但GPU再强也没用，因为GPU之间需要通信。训练一个大模型，不是一块GPU单打独斗，而是几千甚至几万块GPU协同工作。它们需要做梯度同步（gradient all-reduce）、模型层同步、KV缓存交换——这些操作全部依赖网络。

问题在于：GPU性能每年提升10倍，网络交换机每2-3年才提升2-3倍。这就像你买了一辆法拉利，但只能在乡间小路上开。GPU在等网络。网络成了瓶颈。

Drew Perkins说得很直接：“数十亿美元的AI数据中心投资正在被浪费，因为网络跟不上。现有厂商承诺的改进速度，完全不够。”

具体来说，现在的数据中心网络用的是“叶脊架构”（Leaf-Spine）——一层一层的交换机堆叠。需要更多带宽？加更多交换机。但每加一层交换机，数据就多一次“跳转”（hop），延迟就增加一截。而且每一层都需要大量的光模块，光模块是整个网络里最不可靠、最耗电的部分。

结果就是：现在AI训练集群的Scale-up域（一个紧密耦合的GPU组）被限制在大约72块GPU。超过这个规模，网络延迟会急剧增加，GPU利用率断崖式下降。在一个20万GPU的集群里，每提升10%的GPU利用率，就意味着节省超过10亿美元。

Eridu的解决方案，不是在现有架构上修修补补，而是从芯片开始，重新设计整个网络。

核心产品是一个“高基数交换机”（High-Radix Switch）。普通交换机的端口数量有限（比如64个端口），要连接大量GPU就需要很多交换机层层叠加。Eridu的交换机把端口数量提升了一个数量级——一台Eridu交换机可以替代30台传统交换机。

这意味着什么？原来需要三层交换机才能连接的GPU集群，现在一层就够了。数据从一块GPU到另一块GPU，只需要一次跳转，而不是三到五次。延迟大幅降低，功耗降低70%，硬件成本降低40%。

更关键的是规模：Eridu声称它的架构可以让Scale-up域支持数千块GPU（而不是72块），Scale-out域支持超过一百万块GPU。在AI数据中心军备竞赛中，这种量级的差距意味着“能训练出来”和“训练不出来”的区别。

一个62岁连续创业者的“最后一仗”

说说创始人的故事，理解了Drew Perkins这个人，才能理解为什么投资人在产品还没出来时就给了2亿美元。

1963年出生。1986年从卡内基梅隆大学（CMU）毕业，拿了电气工程、计算机工程和数学三个学位。在CMU读书期间，他做了两件事：一是写了CMU PC/IP软件包，让普通的DOS电脑能上互联网；二是作为主要作者起草了PPP协议（RFC 1134/1171）。PPP协议是什么？简单说，在宽带出现之前，全世界的人用拨号上网，拨号上网用的就是PPP。这个协议是互联网基础设施的一部分。 

然后是创业。1998年联合创办Lightera Networks，做光交换，1999年被Ciena以超过5亿美元收购。2000年联合创办OnFiber Communications，做光纤到企业，2006年被Qwest以1.07亿美元收购。2001年联合创办Infinera，做大规模光子集成电路，2007年IPO，2025年被诺基亚以23亿美元收购。2013年联合创办Gainspeed，做有线电视光子网络，被诺基亚收购。2015年联合创办Mojo Vision，做AR隐形眼镜。

五家公司，四次成功退出。退出总金额超过30亿美元。在硅谷，这种“连续创业全胜”的纪录极其罕见。

转折点发生在2023年2月。Perkins和OpenAI的CEO Sam Altman在一个小型会议上聊天。Altman告诉他：“让AI和ChatGPT成为可能的，就是海量的算力。”当时Altman说的是4000块GPU。现在我们说的是百万块GPU。

Perkins立刻意识到：瓶颈不会是芯片本身，而是芯片之间的通信方式。他做了40年的网络基础设施——从PPP到光交换到光子芯片——他比任何人都清楚网络的极限在哪里。

2023年底，他遇到了Omar Hassen。Hassen在Broadcom做过交换机架构师（参与了第一款高密度10G以太网交换机），在Marvell做过产品管理，在AppliedMicro当过VP/GM，还联合创办过半导体公司Entridia。25年以上的网络芯片设计经验。

2024年，两人加上第三位联合创始人Mike Capuano（负责市场），在硅谷Saratoga成立了Eridu。

融资过程很说明问题。Perkins打了几个电话给老朋友——Matter Venture Partners的创始合伙人Wen Hsieh（前凯鹏华盈中国业务负责人）。Hsieh投了，然后把消息告诉了John Doerr（凯鹏华盈传奇投资人，曾投过Perkins之前的公司）。Doerr也要投。然后就变成了抢份额。“我的电话响个不停，”Perkins说。“这轮融资超额认购了很多。”

竞争格局

AI数据中心网络市场目前由几个巨头把持。Broadcom是以太网交换芯片的霸主，Tomahawk 5和Jericho3-AI系列是市场标准，Arista、Cisco等厂商都用Broadcom的芯片。Nvidia既做GPU也做网络，它的InfiniBand（Quantum系列）长期统治高性能计算网络，Spectrum-X系列在以太网侧也有布局。Cisco刚推出了自己的AI网络芯片Silicon One G300。Marvell也在竞争。

大背景是：以太网正在从InfiniBand手中抢回市场。2025年，以太网已经占到AI数据中心后端网络的三分之二以上（此前不到一半）。Ultra Ethernet Consortium（UEC）在2025年6月发布了1.0规范。整个行业在从Nvidia的封闭生态向开放以太网生态转移。

Eridu的定位很独特：它不是在以太网和InfiniBand之间选边站，而是要从硅片层面重新设计交换机。它跟TSMC合作用最先进的工艺和封装技术制造芯片，而不是像Broadcom那样在20年前的架构上迭代改进。目前约100名员工，年底预计170人，其中130人在Saratoga总部。

市场规模：AI数据中心网络市场2026年预计超过500亿美元，到2032年可能达到2000亿美元。这是一个你买GPU的钱越多、你在网络上需要花的钱就越多的市场。

几个值得琢磨的点

先说说“经验”这件事。硅谷最近几年迷恋年轻创始人——20多岁辍学、在宿舍里写代码、三年做到独角兽。这个叙事很性感，但Drew Perkins代表了另一种创业逻辑：做过40年基础设施的人，在看到新需求时，有能力从第一性原理开始重新设计。他不是在学怎么做交换机——他写过互联网协议，他做过光子芯片，他知道物理层的极限在哪里。这种“深度经验”在AI时代反而变成了最稀缺的资源。

再说“网络墙”这个概念。大部分关于AI基础设施的讨论集中在GPU——谁的芯片更快，谁的算力更大。但Eridu指出了一个经常被忽略的事实：GPU的性能已经不是瓶颈了，芯片之间的通信才是。这就像手机行业的演变：当处理器性能足够强了之后，竞争转向了通信协议（5G）、散热、能耗。AI基础设施也在经历同样的转变——从“算力竞赛”转向“连接竞赛”。

然后是“Clean Sheet”（白纸设计）这个策略。Broadcom的交换芯片架构已经迭代了20年，每次改进都在老架构上加东西。Eridu的做法是完全重新设计——新的硅片架构、新的封装技术、新的系统设计。这是一个高风险高回报的赌注：如果成功，可以一步跨越20年的积累；如果失败，2亿美元烧完就没了。

最后是投资人结构。Eridu的投资人名单不是典型的VC名单——它更像是一个“供应链联盟”。TSMC投了（负责芯片制造），MediaTek投了（网络芯片领域的专家），Bosch Ventures和TDK Ventures投了（传感器和电子元器件巨头）。这意味着Eridu从第一天起就在搭建量产所需要的供应链关系。这不是一个PPT公司——它的投资人就是它的供应商。
 

回到开头。一个62岁的网络老兵，1984年开始写互联网底层协议，然后用40年创办了5家公司。2023年2月，他在一个小会议上和Sam Altman聊了一次。Altman说AI靠的是海量算力。Perkins立刻明白：瓶颈不在芯片，在网络。GPU每年快10倍，网络每两三年才快两三倍——这个差距会越来越大，直到整个AI产业被“网络墙”卡住。所以他做了一件在硅谷很老派的事：不是写一个app，不是训练一个模型，而是从芯片开始重新设计网络交换机。一台替代30台，功耗降70%，成本降40%。产品还没发布，TSMC就投了，John Doerr也投了，2亿美元还超额认购了。在一个崇拜20岁创始人的行业里，市场正在给“深度经验”投下最贵的一票。
